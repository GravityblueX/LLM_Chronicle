# May 2025

## Claude 4: From Subscriptions to Usage — A Turning Point in AI Business Models

> Claude Max: Pro $200/month + Max $100/month (usage-based). Plus transparent thinking. In May 2025, Anthropic did not release a "stronger" model — it released a "more expensive" model and an entirely new billing logic.

### Release

**2025-05-22** — Anthropic released **Claude 4**, launching the **Claude Max** subscription plan: Pro $200/month + Max $100/month (usage-based billing). It also introduced the **transparent thinking** feature. [^1]

Claude 4's capabilities ranked among the top across multiple benchmarks — but the real story of this release was not the model; it was the business model.

### Claude Max: The logic of usage-based billing

"Claude Max" was not a traditional fixed monthly subscription. Its billing model was: $200/month base fee (Pro) + up to $100/month in usage-based surcharges. The actual cost depended on how much "extended thinking" you used — more reasoning = higher bill.

This was the first clear signal of the AI industry's transition from "subscription" to "usage-based" pricing. The previous pricing logic was:
- ChatGPT Plus: $20/month, flat rate
- Claude Pro: $20/month, same
- Grok: $30/month, same

All of these models assumed that each user's usage was roughly similar, making a flat price reasonable. But Claude Max explicitly broke this assumption — it said: some users will use a lot (requiring more reasoning), while others will use very little (needing only instant responses). A flat price is too cheap for the former and too expensive for the latter. Usage-based pricing is the only solution.

### Transparent thinking

Like Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Claude 4 continued to support extended thinking mode — users comparing responses from two models without knowing their identities. This time it also included a more complete display of the reasoning process. "Transparent thinking" had become an iconic capability of the Claude series.

### Why a business model shift matters more than a model

2025 was a year of large-scale experimentation with AI business models:
- OpenAI: GPT-4o free + Plus $20 + o1 Pro $200 — three-tier price differentiation
- DeepSeek: API at 1% pricing — developer market share capture driven by ultra-low prices
- Anthropic: Claude Max — from subscription to usage-based

Anthropic's usage-based pricing was the most aggressive experiment. Its premise was that truly heavy users (coding, research, complex analysis) incur reasoning costs far exceeding those of ordinary users — having heavy users pay for their actual usage was the only fair and sustainable approach. If the market accepted this model, it would set a new standard for the entire industry.

### Impact

Claude 4's release deepened the "de-homogenization" trend in the large language model industry. Technically, the differences between it and GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro were growing smaller (gaps measured in decimal points on benchmarks). But the differences in business models were growing larger. When technology competition enters a plateau, business model differentiation becomes the core competitive advantage.

### Commentary

Pairing a business model innovation with a model release — Anthropic's move was shrewd. It sidestepped the increasingly meaningless question of "is Claude 4 stronger than Gemini 2.5 Pro" and instead posed a new one: "How much are you willing to pay for reasoning?"

This was the question that truly defined the AI industry in 2025. Since o1, models had differentiated into two categories: instant responses (cheap, everyday use) and deep reasoning (expensive, professional scenarios). But pricing models had not caught up — most AI services still used a unified monthly fee to cover both usage patterns.

The importance of Claude Max lay not in whether $100/month was expensive — but in making this torn cost structure **explicit**. When the reasoning cost of a "deep thinking" request is dozens of times that of an instant response, a flat monthly fee either forces light users to subsidize heavy users (unfair) or causes the service provider to continuously lose money on heavy users (unsustainable). Usage-based pricing is the only mathematical solution.

Anthropic saw this problem before all its competitors — and was the first to pivot. This was far more significant than a few extra percentage points on any benchmark.
