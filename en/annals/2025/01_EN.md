# January 2025

## DeepSeek-R1: The "DeepSeek Moment" for Open-Source Reasoning Models

> A reasoning model released under the MIT license, with API pricing just one-thirtieth of o1's, wiped $600 billion off NVIDIA's market capitalization and blew open the walled garden of reasoning models.

### Release

**2025-01-20** — DeepSeek (founded by Liang Wenfeng, the creator of High-Flyer Quant) officially released its reasoning model **DeepSeek-R1**, simultaneously open-sourcing the model weights under the MIT license, permitting commercial use and model distillation. The model is based on DeepSeek-V3-Base (MoE architecture, 660B total parameters), specializing in mathematics, code, and logical reasoning tasks, with performance on par with the production version of OpenAI o1. DeepSeek official blog, 2025-01-20[^1]; Wikipedia[^2]

Previously, DeepSeek had released a preview version, **DeepSeek-R1-Lite**, on November 20, 2024, which initially demonstrated the potential of reinforcement learning for reasoning tasks.

### Technical approach: From R1-Zero to R1

DeepSeek-R1's core technical pathway consists of two stages:

**DeepSeek-R1-Zero**: Completely bypasses SFT (supervised fine-tuning) and trains exclusively with **GRPO** (Group Relative Policy Optimization) reinforcement learning. All reward functions are rule-based — accuracy rewards (whether the math answer is correct, whether the code passes tests) and format rewards (whether the chain of thought is placed within `<think>...</think>` tags). No model-based rewards were used.

Pure RL training gave rise to unexpected emergent behaviors: during training, the model spontaneously learned the **"Aha Moment"** — on a given problem, the model would suddenly pause, re-evaluate its own reasoning process in human-readable language, and deliberately allocate more thinking time. The DeepSeek team stated: "This was not programmed in; it developed on its own through the model's interaction with the reinforcement learning environment."

**DeepSeek-R1**: Built upon R1-Zero by introducing cold-start SFT data (several thousand high-quality chain-of-thought examples), followed by RL training. This addressed two problems with R1-Zero: poor output readability (obscure reasoning processes, mixed languages) and unstable initial RL training. The final model maintains reasoning capability while producing clearer, more user-friendly output. DeepSeek-R1 paper, arXiv:2501.12948[^3]

### Performance and cost

| Benchmark | DeepSeek-R1 | R1-Zero | OpenAI o1-1217 |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| AIME 2024 | 79.8% | 71.0% | 79.2% |
| MATH-500 | 97.3% | — | 96.4% |
| Codeforces | 96.3%ile | — | 96.6%ile |
| SWE-bench Verified | 49.2% | — | 48.9% |

**Distilled smaller models**: Through distillation from R1 outputs, six smaller models were released (1.5B to 70B parameters, initialized from Qwen and Llama). Among them, the 32B and 70B models surpassed OpenAI o1-mini on multiple benchmarks, making reasoning capability no longer the exclusive domain of large models.

**API pricing**: 1 yuan per million input tokens (cache hit) / 4 yuan per million input tokens (cache miss), and 16 yuan per million output tokens. The output API price is only approximately **3%** of OpenAI o1's. Cailian Press, 2025-01-28[^4]; Tencent Cloud, 2025-01-23[^5]

**Training cost**: According to the DeepSeek-V3 paper, training used NVIDIA H800 GPUs (a variant specifically produced for the Chinese market), with V3 training costing approximately $5.576 million. External estimates place R1's additional RL training cost slightly higher, totaling around $6 million — a fraction of comparable Western models. Wikipedia[^2]; Voice of America, 2025-01-28[^6]

### Shockwaves

The release of DeepSeek-R1 triggered a chain reaction that extended far beyond the AI community:

- **2025-01-27**: DeepSeek surpassed ChatGPT to reach the top of the free apps chart on the Apple App Store in the United States. The Paper, 2025-01-27[^7]
- **Same day**: NVIDIA's stock price plunged nearly 17%, erasing approximately $589 billion in market value in a single day — the largest single-day loss in U.S. stock market history. The market began reassessing the logic of the "computing power arms race" — DeepSeek proved that top-tier AI capabilities do not necessarily require a top-tier chip budget. Wall Street CN, 2025-01-28[^8]
- **Meta's response**: Mark Zuckerberg announced AI spending exceeding $60 billion for 2025, and Meta internally formed four dedicated teams to study DeepSeek — focusing on cost reduction, training data, and model architecture restructuring, respectively. The Information, 2025-01-27[^9]
- **OpenAI's response**: Claimed to have evidence that DeepSeek used OpenAI's proprietary models to train R1 (a model distillation allegation). DeepSeek responded that it did not use OpenAI model outputs, but acknowledged that, like most generative models, its training inevitably used other AI-generated samples found on the internet. Nature, 2025-09-17[^10]

### Aftermath

- **2025-05-28**: Released **DeepSeek-R1-0528**, a minor update that dramatically improved AIME 2025 accuracy from 70% to 87.5%, with average thinking length increasing from ~12k tokens to ~23k tokens. HuggingFace, 2025-05-28[^11]
- **2025-09-17**: The R1 paper was officially published in **Nature**, becoming one of the first large language model papers to appear in the journal. [^10]
- **Government and industry adoption**: Beginning in February 2025, government departments in Shenzhen, Beijing, Foshan, and other cities integrated DeepSeek; automakers including BMW, BYD, Dongfeng, and Honda announced partnerships. Wikipedia[^2]

### Commentary

DeepSeek-R1 made two contributions. One major, one minor. The minor one is technical; the major one is ecological. But both deserve to be remembered.

**The minor contribution: Pure RL produced reasoning capability.** Before R1-Zero, the industry's default training paradigm was SFT → RLHF — first feeding human-annotated high-quality reasoning examples, then fine-tuning with RL. R1-Zero broke this paradigm: it completely bypassed SFT, starting directly from the base model, and used GRPO reinforcement learning with purely rule-based rewards (correct answer or not, correct format or not) to forge reasoning capability from scratch. Even more striking was the spontaneous emergence of "Aha Moments" during the process — the model taught itself to pause, re-evaluate, and rethink. This was not human-coded behavior; it was an emergent product of the interaction between the RL environment and the model's capabilities. Previously, the emergent phenomena we had witnessed were mostly jumps in benchmark numbers; this time, what emerged was a **behavior**. The philosophical weight of this lies in its implication: reasoning may not necessarily be taught — it can also be *coerced* into existence.

**The major contribution: MIT open-source blew open the walled garden of reasoning models.** If R1 had been yet another closed-source, high-priced API, it would have been merely a competitor to o1 — not an earthquake. OpenAI o1 had turned reasoning models into a luxury: invisible chain of thought, exorbitant pricing, model weights as a black box — an elegant closed loop of "scarce capabilities → premium pricing → computing investment → reinforced barriers." R1 blew this loop open with a single MIT license. Model weights, training techniques, chain-of-thought outputs — all made public. Anyone could download, modify, and distill. The API price was just 3% of o1's. Reasoning models transformed from luxury goods into public goods.

The RL story itself was already compelling enough, but what truly changed the landscape was that decision — **open-sourcing**. Not "forced open-source" (because the results weren't good enough), but "voluntary open-source" (with performance on par with o1). It sent a signal to the entire industry: top-tier AI capabilities need not belong to any single company.

NVIDIA lost $600 billion in a single day; what the market feared was not R1 itself, but the fact it revealed: the most advanced AI does not necessarily require the most GPUs. Computing power is not a moat. The subsequent global wave of reasoning models — Kimi K1.5, Qwen3-Thinker, Claude extended thinking — was catalyzed by R1 without exception. What it defined was not just a model, but an entire category. And the phrase "open-source reasoning model" did not exist before January 20, 2025.
