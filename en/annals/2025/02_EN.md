# February 2025

## GPT-4.5: The End of the Line for Non-Reasoning Models

> Sam Altman called it "the last non-chain-of-thought model." GPT-4.5 lacked o1's reasoning chains and Claude's transparent thinking — it was simply the GPT series reaching a natural endpoint in common sense, conversational naturalness, and knowledge breadth. Then came the declaration: this road ends here; the path forward belongs to reasoning.

### Release

**2025-02-27** — OpenAI released **GPT-4.5** (internal codename Orion). Sam Altman explicitly stated in the release announcement that this was "our last model that isn't a chain-of-thought model" — all future models would uniformly adopt the o-series reasoning chain approach. [^1]

GPT-4.5's design goal was not benchmark breakthroughs — it was to reduce hallucination rates, improve conversational naturalness, and expand knowledge coverage. OpenAI claimed that GPT-4.5's hallucination rate was significantly reduced, with "nonsensically confident outputs" in conversations dropping substantially. On the SimpleQA benchmark, GPT-4.5's accuracy exceeded GPT-4o's; in human preference evaluations, GPT-4.5 also substantially outperformed GPT-4o.

However, GPT-4.5 did not demonstrate significant improvements in reasoning-intensive tasks (mathematics, programming, scientific reasoning) — this domain had already been claimed by the o1/o3 series. Its pricing was also extremely high ($75/million input tokens, $150/million output tokens) — 15 times that of GPT-4o. This made the challenge it faced not merely technical but also economic: when a model is more expensive than existing reasoning models yet weaker at reasoning, who would use it?

### Why it was "the end"

GPT-4.5's positioning was to deliver one final capability upgrade within the GPT series (non-reasoning models). Sam Altman's exact words: "This will be our last model that isn't a chain-of-thought model." This marked OpenAI's formal acknowledgment that the pure "bigger parameters + stronger pre-training" path had reached the stage of diminishing returns. Future growth would come from **test-time compute** rather than **training-time compute**.

This resonated with the rise of DeepSeek-R1 (MIT open-source, one month prior) and o1/o3 — the entire industry completed a paradigm shift from "pre-training scaling" to "reasoning scaling" in early 2025.

### Market reaction

GPT-4.5's release did not create the sensation that GPT-4 or o1 had. The community's general assessment was "too incremental" — GPT-4o had been released only nine months earlier, and GPT-4.5's improvements were not enough to generate excitement. Especially with o1 reasoning models already available, GPT-4.5's extremely high pricing meant its actual user base was very limited.

But GPT-4.5's historical significance lies not in how powerful it was — but in the fact that **it was an obituary**. It heralded the end of an era: from GPT-1 (2018) to GPT-4.5 (2025), a full seven years of the "pre-training scaling" path reached its terminus. The eight-page paper from 2017 declared "Attention Is All You Need" — but in February 2025, Sam Altman was saying: Attention alone is no longer enough.

## Claude 3.7 Sonnet: Anthropic's "Thinking Toggle"

> Anthropic did not separately release a "regular model" and a "reasoning model" — it built both capabilities into the same model. Claude 3.7 Sonnet can operate without thinking (instant response) or switch to extended thinking mode, at the user's discretion. This was a paradigm-level rebuttal to the o1 approach.

### Release

**2025-02-24** (three days before GPT-4.5) — Anthropic released **Claude 3.7 Sonnet**, the first **hybrid reasoning model**. [^2]

Claude 3.7 Sonnet's core innovation was its treatment of "reasoning" not as a separate model category (like OpenAI's o-series), but as an **optional mode** embedded within the same model:

- **Instant response mode**: Similar to Claude 3.5 Sonnet, standard conversational speed
- **Extended Thinking mode**: Users can have the model perform internal reasoning before responding, and the model displays its thinking process — not o1-style "fully hidden," but **transparent thinking**

On coding benchmarks, Claude 3.7 Sonnet's score on SWE-bench (62.3%) dramatically surpassed Claude 3.5 Sonnet's (40.6%) — Anthropic positioned it as primarily targeting developers and complex tasks.

### Transparent thinking vs. hidden chains of thought

This design choice was a paradigm-level rebuttal to OpenAI's o1. When o1 launched in September 2024, it hid the chain of thought, reasoning that this helped prevent users from learning to circumvent safety restrictions. But DeepSeek-R1 (January 2025), with its MIT open-source release and public chain of thought, directly challenged o1 — demonstrating that "making the reasoning process public does not necessarily lead to safety problems."

Anthropic's decision to let Claude 3.7 Sonnet show its thinking process clearly drew lessons from both sides: OpenAI's concealment strategy triggered a trust crisis, while DeepSeek-R1's transparency won community respect. Claude 3.7 Sonnet's transparent thinking represented a "third way" between these two strategies — it said: you don't need to hide the chain of thought to ensure safety; you just need to design a model that is safe enough.

### Impact

Claude 3.7 Sonnet consolidated Anthropic's reputation in the programming domain — its SWE-bench score led all models of its generation. More importantly, its "hybrid reasoning" paradigm — one model, two modes — opened a new path distinct from OpenAI's o-series. Subsequently, Qwen 3 (April 2025) adopted a similar "thinking/non-thinking" dual-mode design.

### Commentary

GPT-4.5 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet were released within three days of each other in February 2025 — this was not a coincidence but a reflection of accelerating competitive cadence. Yet the underlying narratives of the two releases were diametrically opposed.

GPT-4.5 was an obituary: it declared the pre-training era over and the reasoning chain approach as the future. Claude 3.7 Sonnet was a manifesto: it declared that "reasoning" should not be a separate model category — it should be a switch, toggled by the user whenever needed.

The tension between these two narratives ran through the entire model competition of 2025. OpenAI chose to keep GPT and o separate — having users switch between different models. Anthropic chose to merge them — having users switch between different modes of the same model. The former is simpler in product terms (each model does one thing) but more complex technically (maintaining two product lines). The latter is more complex in product terms (requiring users to understand what "extended thinking" means) but simpler technically (only one model to maintain).

Interestingly, subsequent entrants generally followed Anthropic's approach: Qwen 3 supported "thinking/non-thinking" modes, and Gemini 2.5 Pro also featured reasoning enhancement within a single model. This suggests the industry consensus is shifting from "separation" toward "unification" — and Claude 3.7 Sonnet was the starting point of that shift.
