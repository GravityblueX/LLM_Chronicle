# August 2025

## GPT-5: OpenAI's Fifth-Generation Base Model

> GPT-5 was not a technical revolution — it was OpenAI's attempt, after GPT-4, GPT-4o, GPT-4.5, o1, and o3, to finally merge all technical lines into a single model. "Unification" itself was the product's greatest narrative.

### Release

**2025-08-07** — OpenAI released **GPT-5** via a livestream, simultaneously launching on ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, and the OpenAI API. [^1]

GPT-5 was the fifth-generation base model in the GPT series, preceded by GPT-4 (March 2023), GPT-4o (May 2024), and GPT-4.5 (February 2025, internal codename Orion, originally intended as GPT-5 but downgraded due to results falling short of expectations). [^2]

### Technical highlights

According to OpenAI, GPT-5's core improvements included:

- **Unified reasoning mode**: Built-in "router" that automatically determines whether a task requires deep reasoning. Simple questions are answered directly (fast mode); complex questions trigger a "thinking longer" reasoning chain — no manual o1/o3 mode selection required by the user. [^1]
- **Safe Completions**: A new safety policy — instead of outright refusal of potentially harmful queries, the model attempts to provide safe, high-level responses. OpenAI stated this policy caused GPT-5 to "refuse more truly unsafe questions while refusing less harmless information." [^1]
- **Reduced sycophantic responses**: GPT-5 was trained to be less "sycophantic" than its predecessors — providing more direct and more critical answers. [^1]
- **Lower hallucination rates**: In multiple accuracy tests, GPT-5's hallucination rate was significantly lower than GPT-4o and GPT-4.5. [^1]

### Performance

At launch, GPT-5 achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) on benchmarks in mathematics, programming, finance, and multimodal understanding. Sam Altman described it on the eve of release as "a significant step on the path to AGI" with "PhD-level capabilities." [^1]

### Industry context

GPT-5's release followed closely on the heels of the "reasoning model wars" of early 2025 — OpenAI o3-mini (January 2025), DeepSeek-R1 (January 2025), and Claude 4 (May 2025) had all launched in succession. GPT-5's core strategy was not "stronger" but "unified" — merging the previously split GPT conversational line and o-series reasoning line into a single model, with a router handling automatic dispatch. This addressed a user pain point: o1/o3 required manual switching, GPT-4o was not good at reasoning, and GPT-5 attempted to bring both worlds together.

Notably, two days before GPT-5's release (2025-08-05), OpenAI also open-sourced **GPT-OSS** — two open-weight models with reasoning capabilities. [^1] This was a rare open-source move by OpenAI, widely seen as a response to DeepSeek's open-source strategy.
