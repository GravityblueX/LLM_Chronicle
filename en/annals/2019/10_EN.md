# October 2019

## T5: Everything Is Text-to-Text

> Converting all NLP tasks into "input text → output text." Translation, question answering, summarization, classification — all using the same format, the same model, the same loss function. T5's ambition was not to set new benchmarks, but to eliminate task-specific architecture design entirely.

### Release

**2019-10** — Google Research published the paper "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer," introducing **T5** (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer) and releasing its largest variant at 11B parameters.[^1]

T5's central proposition was a **unified framework**: all NLP tasks — translation, summarization, question answering, text classification, natural language inference — were reformulated into an "input text → output text" format. For example:

- Translation: `"translate English to German: That is good." → "Das ist gut."`
- Summarization: `"summarize: [long text]" → "[summary]"`
- Sentiment analysis: `"sentence: [sentence] sentiment:" → "positive"`

A single model architecture (Transformer encoder-decoder), a single pre-training objective (span corruption — randomly masking text spans and having the model reconstruct them), and a single loss function.

### Technical contributions

Beyond the unified framework, T5's primary contribution was a massive **systematic ablation study** — much of the paper's 67 pages were devoted to answering "which design choices actually matter?":

- **Pre-training objective**: span corruption > language model > BERT-style masking
- **Data**: trained on C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus, 750GB of text cleaned from Common Crawl)
- **Architecture**: the original Transformer encoder-decoder outperformed decoder-only or encoder-only variants
- **Scale**: the 11B-parameter T5 set new state-of-the-art on 17 of 24 tasks

The paper also introduced the **C4 dataset**, which became one of the most widely used pre-training corpora in NLP.

### Impact

T5's "Text-to-Text" philosophy profoundly influenced subsequent LLM development — methods such as instruction tuning, FLAN, and T0 all essentially reformulate tasks into natural language instruction text, inheriting T5's unified framework approach. Although T5's encoder-decoder architecture was later overtaken by decoder-only architectures in the GPT era, the intuition that "everything can be text-to-text" has been absorbed into the very DNA of prompt engineering and instruction tuning.

### Commentary

T5 was Google's systematic answer to the question: "What is the correct form of the pre-training paradigm?" BERT proved that pre-training works, GPT-2 proved that scaling works — T5 asked a more fundamental question on top of both: **what should the framework itself look like?** The answer: a sufficiently general interface, combined with a sufficiently good pre-training objective, could cover most NLP tasks.

However, at the commercial and industry level, T5's influence was less than that of GPT-2 and BERT — partly because it arrived late (GPT-3 was released seven months later, overshadowing all discussion at the 11B scale with 175B and few-shot capabilities), and partly because its encoder-decoder architecture was not well-suited for free-form text generation. T5's legacy lies more in methodology — unified frameworks, large-scale ablations, clean dataset engineering — things that successors could draw upon again and again.
