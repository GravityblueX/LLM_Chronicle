# June 2019

## XLNet: A Last Stand for Permutation Language Modeling

> Eight months after BERT's dominion over the pre-training world, XLNet used "permutation language modeling" to comprehensively outperform it across 20 benchmarks. But architectural elegance ultimately could not withstand the pragmatism of the GPT trajectory — this was the encoder-decoder approach's zenith, and its farewell.

### Release

**2019-06** — Yang et al. from CMU and Google Brain released **XLNet**, proposing **Permutation Language Modeling**. With 340M parameters, comparable in scale to BERT-large, it surpassed BERT across all 20 NLP benchmarks.[^1]

XLNet's core innovation was combining the strengths of both BERT and GPT: it bidirectional context like BERT, but with an autoregressive objective like GPT — by treating all permutations of the input as training sequences, each token could attend to every other token when predicting itself. Additionally, XLNet integrated Transformer-XL's segment-level recurrence mechanism, enabling the model to handle longer-range dependencies.

### Why it never became mainstream

Despite comprehensively outperforming BERT on benchmarks, XLNet did not replace BERT, let alone derail the GPT trajectory. The reasons: permutation language modeling incurs high training costs (attention masks must be recomputed for each permutation), is less straightforward than BERT at inference time, and lacks GPT's generative capabilities. It was a technically elegant solution, but one that addressed more of an elegant question — "how to simultaneously achieve bidirectional understanding and autoregressive generation" — rather than a problem that real users actually needed solved.

### Commentary

XLNet was the pinnacle of the BERT trajectory — it was unrivaled in the direction of "how to better pre-train a Transformer encoder." But in the very same year that XLNet was released, OpenAI had already begun scaling GPT-2 from 355M to 1.5B. Technical elegance would eventually be leveled by brute-force scaling. The BERT trajectory continued after XLNet with RoBERTa, ALBERT, and ELECTRA — but each successive contribution offered diminishing returns. The GPT trajectory, meanwhile, was only beginning to accelerate.
