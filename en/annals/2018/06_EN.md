# June 2018

## GPT-1: The First Brick of Generative Pre-Training

> 117M parameters, a 12-page paper, a simple formula — GPT-1 wasn't flashy, but it proved a path that would later transform the entire AI industry: first train a generative model on large amounts of unlabeled text, then fine-tune on specific tasks. This path, followed to this day, is called ChatGPT.

### Release

**2018-06-11** — Alec Radford and other OpenAI researchers published "Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training," introducing GPT-1.[^1]

GPT stands for **G**enerative **P**re-**T**raining, a name that would continue through the entire series. GPT-1 had only 117M parameters, 12 Transformer decoder layers, 768-dimensional hidden layers, and 12 attention heads. The training data was BooksCorpus — a dataset containing approximately 7,000 unpublished books.[^2]

The paper's structure was surprisingly simple:
1. **Pre-training**: Train on BooksCorpus using standard language model objective (predict next word)
2. **Fine-tuning**: Add a task-specific linear layer for specific tasks (text entailment, question answering, sentiment analysis, semantic similarity) and continue training

Results: Achieved state-of-the-art on 9 of 12 benchmarks. This included models specifically designed for each task using LSTM architectures.

### The Fork with BERT

GPT-1 and BERT were published in the same year, both proposing the "pre-training + fine-tuning" paradigm, but took two entirely different paths:

| | GPT-1 | BERT |
|---|---|---|
| **Architecture** | Transformer decoder | Transformer encoder |
| **Direction** | Unidirectional (left→right), predict next word | Bidirectional, masked prediction |
| **Natural Strength** | Text generation | Text understanding |
| **Pre-training Task** | Standard language model | MLM + NSP |
| **Ambition** | One model for all NLP tasks | Refresh SOTA on all understanding tasks |

GPT-1 planted seeds on the "generation" path. Its decoder architecture meant the model was naturally good at writing — this would be validated again and again in GPT-2/GPT-3/ChatGPT. BERT won the benchmark war in 2018 (11 SOTAs), but the GPT path won the industry war in 2022 — because people ultimately discovered that a model that "can write" is far more useful than one that "can read."

### Why It Was Underestimated

GPT-1 didn't cause the sensation that BERT did when released. The reason isn't complicated: 117M parameters, results only better than pre-BERT state-of-the-art on 9/12 tasks. BERT crushed everything four months later with 340M parameters and 11 SOTAs. From the perspective of the time, GPT-1 seemed more like an exploratory work of "this could work too" rather than a manifesto for a new paradigm.

But in retrospect, GPT-1's paper contained an overlooked sentence: "We believe that significant improvements in language understanding can be achieved through generative pre-training at scale." The "at scale" is key — GPT-1's authors knew from the beginning that the core of this path was scaling. They just didn't have the resources to validate what 175B parameters would look like.

### Impact

GPT-1 was the starting point of the GPT series — its decoder architecture, pre-training + fine-tuning paradigm, and BooksCorpus data selection all continued in GPT-2/GPT-3, with only scale changing. If measured by "impact on posterity / attention received at release," GPT-1 may be one of the most severely underestimated papers in AI history.

### Commentary

GPT-1's situation is somewhat like a pioneer overwhelmed by successors. BERT stole all the headlines and benchmarks four months later, but GPT-1's "unidirectional decoder + language model pre-training" path ultimately proved to go further than BERT's "bidirectional encoder" path — because generation capabilities naturally support dialogue, translation, code, and search, while understanding capabilities only have advantages in specific discriminative tasks.

History's evaluation of technology trees often lags by several years. GPT-1 was an "interesting exploration" in 2018, but became "prophetic foundation" by 2022. Not because the paper's content changed — but because GPT-2, GPT-3, and ChatGPT step by step proved how far this path could go. The 117M small model was unremarkable in the context of 2018, but that simple formula of "pre-training + fine-tuning" was ultimately amplified 1,500 times and changed the world.
