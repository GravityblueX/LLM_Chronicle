# June 2017

## Transformer: "Attention Is All You Need"

> An eight-page paper, eight authors, an engine that changed the world. Transformer is not a model — it is a key that opened the door to all subsequent large language models.

### Paper Publication

**2017-06-12** — "Attention Is All You Need" was published on arXiv. Eight authors from Google Brain and Google Research: Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Łukasz Kaiser, Illia Polosukhin.[^1]

This paper proposed a completely new neural network architecture — **Transformer** — that completely abandoned the mainstream RNN (Recurrent Neural Network) and LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) approaches of the time, relying solely on a mechanism called "Self-Attention" to process sequential data. The title said it boldly: "Attention Is All You Need" — attention is all you need.

At that time, the standard approach in NLP was to use RNN/LSTM to process sentences word by word, combined with attention mechanisms to capture long-distance dependencies between words. But this approach had two fatal flaws: first, RNNs could not perform parallel computation — they had to process sequentially, where the vector for the 100th word had to wait for all 99 previous words to be computed; second, when sentences became too long, RNNs would naturally "forget" earlier information.

Transformer bypassed both problems. Its multi-head self-attention mechanism allowed each word to directly "converse" with all other words in the sentence, computing relationships between all words at once — no sequential order, only attention weights. This enabled efficient parallel training while naturally solving the long-distance dependency problem.

### Architectural Highlights

The Transformer in the paper is an encoder-decoder structure used for machine translation tasks. However, the vast majority of subsequent large language models used only its decoder part (Decoder-only), including all GPT series, Llama series, Gemini series, etc.

Core components:

- **Multi-Head Self-Attention**: Projects inputs into multiple "attention heads," where each head captures relationships between words from different angles. All heads compute in parallel, and results are concatenated. This allows the model to simultaneously capture syntactic structure, semantic associations, reference relationships, and other levels of information.
- **Positional Encoding**: Since self-attention has no concept of "order" (to the model, "I hit you" and "you hit me" have the same attention matrix), positional information must be injected separately. The paper used sine/cosine functions to generate fixed positional encodings, while later work (like BERT) switched to learnable position embeddings.
- **Residual Connection + Layer Norm**: Residual connections from ResNet allow gradients to flow smoothly through deep networks, while layer normalization stabilizes training.
- **Scaled Dot-Product Attention**: The core attention formula QK^T / √d_k. Dividing by √d_k prevents the dot product from becoming too large and causing softmax gradient vanishing.

The paper's model was divided into base (65M parameters) and big (213M parameters). By today's standards, this is laughably small — GPT-3 has 175B parameters, more than 800 times larger than Transformer big. But the elegance of the architecture allows it to scale to virtually any size with minimal modifications.

The subsequent paths of the eight Transformer authors are worth noting, as they reveal the landscape shifts across the AI industry over five years:

- **Ashish Vaswani** — Co-founded Adept AI (later renamed Character AI), then founded Essential AI
- **Noam Shazeer** — Founded Character.AI, valued at over $1 billion
- **Niki Parmar** — Co-founded Essential AI
- **Jakob Uszkoreit** — Founded Inceptive, focusing on AI + biology
- **Llion Jones** — Left Google to found Sakana AI (Japan), focusing on evolutionary AI
- **Aidan N. Gomez** — Co-founded Cohere (Canada), enterprise LLM

Eight people, seven left Google to start companies, most successfully. A paper's author group ultimately scattered into seven or eight AI startups — this "paper → entrepreneurship" fission pattern would repeat throughout the subsequent AI industry.

### Why It Was "The Beginning of Everything"

After Transformer's publication, NLP experienced a "slow detonation" process:

- In 2018, OpenAI used the Transformer decoder architecture to create **GPT-1** — the "generative pre-training + fine-tuning" path
- At the end of 2018, Google used the Transformer encoder architecture to create **BERT** — the bidirectional understanding "pre-training + fine-tuning" path
- From 2019-2020, OpenAI raced along the GPT path, GPT-2 → GPT-3, with scale increasing ten to a hundredfold each generation
- In 2020, GPT-3's few-shot emergent abilities made the entire industry realize: Transformer's potential was far from exhausted

Then came what everyone knows. At the end of 2022, ChatGPT launched, transforming Transformer from a research architecture into an industrial engine.

Notably, the core innovation in the Transformer paper — self-attention — was not conceptually entirely new. In 2014, Bahdanau et al. had already used attention mechanisms in RNN machine translation; in 2015, Luong et al. expanded it. Transformer's innovation wasn't "inventing attention," but "throwing away RNN and using only attention." This was a subtractive insight — sometimes knowing what to remove is harder than knowing what to add.

### Impact

Transformer's impact extended beyond NLP. Its self-attention mechanism was subsequently applied to:

- **Computer Vision** (ViT, 2020) — splitting images into patches and processing image blocks like words
- **Protein Structure Prediction** (AlphaFold 2, 2020) — using attention to capture spatial relationships between amino acids
- **Audio Processing** (Whisper, MusicGen) — treating audio frames as token sequences
- **Multimodal Models** (CLIP, DALL·E 2, GPT-4V, Gemini) — text and images sharing attention space
- **Code Generation** (Codex, Copilot) — code is also a sequence

By 2026, virtually every AI model with significant impact was built on Transformer or its variants. "Attention Is All You Need" transformed from a paper title into a statement about an era.

### Commentary

Eight pages, eight authors, one engine. Transformer's brilliance lies not in complexity — the architectural core is only six encoder layers plus six decoder layers, with the paper clearly marking input and output dimensions for each layer — but in its elegant solution to a fundamental problem: how to let a machine see an entire sentence at once, rather than reading word by word.

"Knowing what to remove" is where this architecture's true genius lies. Throw away RNN, throw away convolution, keep only attention. This subtractive insight is uncommon in AI history. Most of the time, our approach to solving problems is "add another layer," "add another module," "add another loss term." Transformer went the opposite direction, resulting in something faster, stronger, and simpler.

All the large model stories of the following decade — GPT's expansion, BERT's explosion, ChatGPT's breakthrough — all began with this eight-page paper. It's not a product, not a company, not a launch. It's a mathematical structure, and the courage to write that structure clearly.
