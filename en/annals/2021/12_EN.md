# December 2021

## Gopher: DeepMind's Scaling Law Validation

### Release

**2021-12** — DeepMind released **Gopher**, a 280B-parameter model — one of the largest dense language models at the time. The paper conducted a systematic evaluation across 152 tasks, marking DeepMind's first large-scale foray into LLMs.[^1]

Gopher's core contribution was the systematic validation of **scaling laws**: across 152 tasks spanning knowledge, reasoning, mathematics, code, dialogue, and other domains, model performance continued to improve with increasing parameter scale. On knowledge-intensive tasks in particular (such as MMLU), the 280B Gopher substantially outperformed the 7.1B version, confirming the applicability of the scaling laws proposed by Kaplan et al. in 2020 to large models.

### Interesting findings in the paper

- **Reading comprehension improved most with scale**: from 7.1B to 280B, reading comprehension showed the greatest gains, suggesting that "more knowledge comes from more parameters"
- **Mathematical reasoning improved least**: even the 280B Gopher performed poorly on elementary math problems — indicating that "reasoning" cannot be solved simply by adding parameters
- **Toxicity increased with scale**: larger models were more likely to generate harmful content when producing text — this provided empirical motivation for subsequent work on RLHF and Constitutional AI

### Impact

Gopher itself did not become a widely adopted model (DeepMind's LLM strategy later pivoted to Gemini), but its systematic evaluation methodology and scaling law validation paved the way for Chinchilla (March 2022, a follow-up from the same team). Chinchilla further proposed the finding that "data and parameters should grow proportionally," correcting a deficiency in the Kaplan scaling laws.

### Commentary

Gopher was DeepMind's "belated declaration" in the LLM race. By late 2021, OpenAI's GPT-3 had been out for a year and a half, Anthropic had recently been founded but was already training Claude, and Google internally had LaMDA and PaLM in progress. DeepMind had previously focused on non-linguistic domains like AlphaGo and AlphaFold — Gopher was its first serious engagement with LLMs.

Two findings in the paper are more worth remembering than the model itself. First, the systematic evaluation across 152 tasks confirmed the applicability of scaling laws at extreme scale — an important independent validation of the Kaplan laws. Second, the negative findings that "reasoning capability does not improve linearly with scale" and "toxicity increases with scale" were actually more valuable than the positive results — they directly guided DeepMind's subsequent work on Chinchilla (proportional data-parameter growth) and Sparrow (RLHF alignment).

From an industry perspective, Gopher was DeepMind's starting line in the LLM race. Within a year, DeepMind and Google Brain merged into Google DeepMind, and Chinchilla and Gemini followed in succession. Gopher itself was overshadowed, but the questions it raised and the methodologies it validated cast shadows across every subsequent DeepMind model.
