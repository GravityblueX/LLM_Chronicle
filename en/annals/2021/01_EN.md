# January 2021

## DALL·E: When the Transformer Learned to Draw

> Text-to-image generation — not searching an image library, but creating images from scratch based on descriptions. DALL·E used a 12B-parameter GPT-3 architecture combined with VQ-VAE to give AI a visual imagination. Though the image quality was still crude, the door it opened led to DALL·E 2, Stable Diffusion, and Midjourney.

### Release

**2021-01-05** — OpenAI released **DALL·E** (named after Dalí + WALL-E), a 12B-parameter text-to-image generation model based on GPT-3.[^1]

DALL·E's technical approach was distinctive: it was not a diffusion model (that would come with DALL·E 2 in 2022), but rather treated images as a kind of "text token." Specifically:

- A VQ-VAE compressed 256×256 images into a 32×32 grid of discrete tokens
- A GPT-3-style Transformer received both text tokens and image tokens, autoregressively generating the image token sequence
- Finally, the VQ-VAE decoder reconstructed the token grid back into an image

This approach of "using a language model for visual generation" was essentially a language model — having learned to caption images — doing the reverse: "listening to descriptions and drawing."

### Capability demonstrations

DALL·E showcased several capabilities that were astonishing at the time:
- **Zero-shot visual reasoning**: "an armchair in the shape of an avocado" — the model could simultaneously understand the shape of an "avocado" and the function of an "armchair," fusing the two into a novel object
- **Multi-object composition**: placing multiple objects with specified spatial positions, colors, and material constraints
- **Style control**: changing rendering style according to instructions like "in the style of a pencil drawing" or "like a stained-glass window"
- **Uncommon concepts**: generating never-before-seen combinations like "a harp made of snails" or "a cup made of porcupines"

The model was only a proof of concept and was not made available to the public. But it conclusively demonstrated that Transformer sequence modeling could extend into pixel space.

### Switch Transformer: the sparse gateway to trillion parameters

**2021-01** — In the same month, Google Brain released **Switch Transformer**, with a parameter count reaching 1.6 trillion — the first model to break the trillion-parameter barrier. Its core innovation was a simplified version of **Mixture of Experts (MoE)** — each token activated only a single "expert" sub-network, leaving most parameters dormant during inference. This allowed the model's total parameter count to be enormous while keeping actual computational costs manageable.[^2]

Switch Transformer demonstrated that sparse MoE was a viable scaling strategy — when dense models hit the wall of computational cost, sparse activation could circumvent this bottleneck. This trajectory was later inherited and developed by models like Mixtral (2023) and DeepSeek-V2/V3 (2024).

### Commentary

January 2021 was a fork in the road. On the left, DALL·E — using Transformers to generate pixels, AI stepping from the linguistic world into the visual world. On the right, Switch Transformer — using MoE to scale parameters to the trillions, scaling shifting from dense to sparse. At the time, these two developments seemed unrelated, but viewed along a longer timeline, they answered the same question: **where are the boundaries of the Transformer?** DALL·E said: not within language alone — vision works too. Switch Transformer said: the physical limits of dense models can be overcome with sparsity. Over the next five years, the combination of multimodality + MoE appeared in virtually every generation of frontier models — GPT-4 (multimodal MoE), Gemini (natively multimodal), DeepSeek-V3 (MoE) — the two threads of January 2021 ultimately converged.
