# December 2023

## Gemini 1.0: Google finally fights back head-on

> Outperforming GPT-4 on 30 out of 32 benchmarks — this was the answer Google DeepMind delivered in December 2023. But an edited demo video cast a shadow over that report card.

### Release

**2023-12-06** — Google DeepMind officially released **Gemini 1.0** in three tiers:[^1]

| Version | Positioning | Capability |
|------|------|------|
| **Ultra** | Strongest, competing with GPT-4 | Leading in 30/32 benchmarks |
| **Pro** | Mid-tier, powering Google products | Bard upgraded to Gemini Pro |
| **Nano** | On-device, running on phones | Deployed on Pixel 8 Pro, works offline |

Gemini's core selling point was **native multimodality** — trained jointly on text, images, audio, video, and code from its design inception. This differed from GPT-4's "vision added later" approach — GPT-4V had image understanding capability added after GPT-4's training was already complete.

### The demo controversy

Gemini's release was accompanied by a stunning hands-on demo video: the model understood the user's drawings, gestures, and magic tricks in real time, engaging in natural voice conversation. The video accumulated millions of views within hours of release.

But attentive viewers quickly noticed: the video was annotated with "For the purposes of this demo, latency has been reduced and outputs have been shortened." Bloomberg's further investigation revealed that the interactions in the video were actually **static images + text prompts**, not real-time video understanding — Google had edited multiple independent text interactions into an illusion of "real-time conversation."[^2]

Google acknowledged this and stated the video was produced to "inspire developers." But it seriously damaged the credibility of Gemini's launch. The community's general reaction was: "If Gemini is truly this strong, why does it need editing to prove it?"

### Genuine technical advances

Demo controversy aside, Gemini 1.0 did bring some real technical breakthroughs:

- **MMLU benchmark**: Gemini Ultra achieved 90.0% on MMLU, becoming the first model to exceed 90% and the first to surpass human experts (89.8%) — this figure later became an important yardstick for measuring "frontier models"
- **Native multimodality**: Gemini's training data included interleaved sequences of images, audio, video, and text, rather than post-hoc stitching. This gave it a natural advantage in complex tasks requiring cross-modal understanding — such as analyzing a lecture video and answering specific questions about it
- **TPU v5p**: Gemini was trained on Google's latest-generation TPU, marking Google's self-sufficiency in hardware

### Google's "Bard → Gemini" brand overhaul

Gemini's release marked a complete transformation of Google's AI assistant brand:

- **2023-02**: Google launched Bard, based on LaMDA, hastily responding to ChatGPT
- **2023-05**: Bard upgraded to PaLM 2
- **2023-12**: Bard upgraded to Gemini Pro
- **2024-02**: Bard officially renamed **Gemini**, with the launch of Gemini Advanced (Ultra 1.0)

From a "hastily assembled ChatGPT competitor" to a "product with clear branding and technical roadmap," Gemini took one year to complete the transition.

### Aftermath

- **2024-02-15**: Gemini 1.5 Pro — 1M token context window, capable of processing the entirety of *War and Peace* in a single pass
- **2024-12**: Gemini 2.0 Flash — pioneer of the "agentic era," faster, cheaper, with native tool calling support
- **2025-03**: Gemini 2.5 Pro — a thinking model, reaching the top of LMArena
- **2026-05**: Gemini 3.1 Pro / 3 Deep Think / 3.5 Flash — continuous iteration

### Impact

After Gemini's launch, the large model competition officially became a three-way contest: **OpenAI vs Google DeepMind vs Anthropic.** Each had unique assets: OpenAI had first-mover advantage and brand, Google had search data and TPU hardware, and Anthropic had its safety reputation.

More importantly, Gemini proved that **multimodality was the default path, not an option.** Before Gemini, "multimodal" was an add-on feature of GPT-4V. After Gemini, every newly released frontier model was multimodal by default — from the design stage.

### Commentary

Gemini 1.0's release was a watershed — not because of the 30 benchmarks it surpassed GPT-4 on, but because it marked Google's final departure from the passive posture of "hastily responding to ChatGPT" and its entry into the active phase of "advancing AI at its own pace."

Throughout 2023, Google's position was awkward. It possessed the deepest AI research legacy in the world — the Transformer was Google's invention, AlphaGo was Google's, TPU was Google's. But after ChatGPT launched, Google looked like a follower — Bard was hastily released, the demo error caused Alphabet's stock to plummet. A pervasive sense of insecurity within the company was repeatedly amplified by Wall Street and Silicon Valley.

Gemini 1.0 attempted to reverse this narrative. It chose a smart positioning — not pursuing "is the conversational experience better than ChatGPT," but pursuing "are the benchmark scores higher than GPT-4." The 30/32 lead was a number that fit in a headline. And native multimodality was a differentiating capability that ChatGPT did not yet possess (GPT-4V was not released until 2023-09 and was not available to all users).

But the demo fabrication incident exposed a deeper problem: when competitive pressure is intense enough, even a company like Google will choose "capturing attention" over "staying honest." Gemini's demo and Google's other product releases of the same era (such as the frequent use of "coming soon" instead of "available now" at Google I/O) formed a pattern: **demo first, product later.** This is a common predicament for large companies in the AI race — when competitors are releasing new things every week, a "real but unflashy" demo cannot compete in the attention market with an "edited but flashy" one.

Ultimately, Gemini's technical prowess was vindicated. 1.5 Pro's 1M token context, 2.5 Pro's reasoning ability — these were genuine advances. But the edited video of December 2023 will remain as a footnote in AI history about "how hard it is to stay honest under pressure."
