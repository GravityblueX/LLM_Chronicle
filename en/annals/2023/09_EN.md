# September 2023

## Mistral 7B: The 7B underdog strikes back

> A French startup just seven months old, with a 7.3B-parameter model, surpassed the 13B Llama 2 on multiple benchmarks. Apache 2.0 open source. Europe finally had an AI card to play.

### Release

**2023-09-27** — French AI startup **Mistral AI** released **Mistral 7B**. 7.3B parameters, Apache 2.0 license. The release method was simple to the point of being provocative: a magnet link, dropped on Twitter.[^1]

Mistral 7B's architecture featured several key innovations:
- **Grouped-Query Attention (GQA)**: Sharing Key/Value projections across attention heads, dramatically reducing memory usage during inference
- **Sliding Window Attention (SWA)**: Covering long sequences with a fixed-size window, making inference cost linearly related to sequence length
- These designs gave the 7B Mistral far superior inference speed and memory efficiency compared to models of equivalent scale

The result: Mistral 7B surpassed the 13B Llama 2 across multiple reasoning, math, and code benchmarks. The community's reaction was one of shock — "small models can beat large models" had already been validated once in the Llama 1 era, but Mistral pushed this "efficiency miracle" to a new extreme.

### Why it mattered

Mistral AI's three co-founders — Arthur Mensch, Guillaume Lample, and Timothée Lacroix — all came from DeepMind and Meta AI. One of their motivations for leaving big companies to found Mistral was the belief that Europe needed its own AI infrastructure.

Mistral 7B proved one thing: **in the LLM field, "small team + good engineering decisions" can beat "big team + more compute."** 7.3B parameters surpassing 13B — this was not achieved by piling on GPUs, but through smarter attention mechanisms, cleaner training data, and meticulous engineering optimization.

Subsequently, Mistral released Mixtral 8×7B (2023-12, the first high-quality open-source MoE) and Mistral Large 2 (2024-07, a 123B model competing with GPT-4). But Mistral 7B remained their most symbolically significant product: it represented Europe's AI declaration of independence — "We don't need Silicon Valley's permission to train a good model."

### Commentary

A magnet link, dropped on Twitter, 7.3B parameters, surpassing the 13B Llama 2. Mistral 7B's release style was itself a statement: AI does not need the grandeur of tech giants, no launch events, no blog posts. A good model speaks for itself.

But Mistral's story has a deeper layer. The three co-founders came from DeepMind and Meta — Europe's brightest AI researchers chose to return to Europe to start a company rather than remain in Silicon Valley. This was not just a technical choice; it was a civilizational one. Europe missed the platform dividends of the internet era (having no Google of its own, no Facebook of its own), but in the AI era, Mistral proved that Europe could build frontier models — not requiring the most GPUs, but requiring the smartest engineering.

After Mistral 7B, the "small model miracle" became a new narrative archetype — demonstrating that parameter efficiency matters more than parameter count. Microsoft's Phi series, Apple's OpenELM, and Google's Gemma all followed down the same path. But Mistral 7B was the first — and the first is always remembered.
