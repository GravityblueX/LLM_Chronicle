# July 2023

## Llama 2: Open source transforms from accident to strategy

> Four months ago, Llama was leaked. Four months later, Meta voluntarily released Llama 2 — fully open-source, commercially permitted, distributed through Microsoft Azure. From "forced openness" to "voluntary openness," Zuckerberg needed only 145 days.

### Release

**2023-07-18** — Meta and Microsoft jointly released **Llama 2** in three sizes: 7B, 13B, and 70B. All versions came with pre-trained base models and instruction-tuned variants (Llama 2-Chat).[^1]

The key change: **the open-source license permitted commercial and research use.** This was completely different from Llama 1's "research use only by approved institutions." Although the license still contained some restrictions (entities with over 700 million monthly active users needed additional permission), this was the first truly "commercially usable open-source large language model."

Another important strategic move: **distribution through Microsoft partnership.** Llama 2 could not only be downloaded through Meta's channels but also deployed directly via the Azure AI catalog — a deeply meaningful signal for the AI industry: Microsoft was simultaneously betting on OpenAI (closed-source) and Meta (open-source). "We're no longer betting on just one direction" — this was the strategy Microsoft showed through actions rather than words.

### Training and performance

- Training data: 2T tokens, a 40% increase over Llama 1
- Context window: 4,096 tokens (Llama 1 was 2,048)
- The 70B version achieved 68.9% on MMLU — while still below GPT-4's 86.4%, it matched GPT-3.5 and was fully open-source and commercially usable
- Llama 2-Chat 70B roughly matched ChatGPT (GPT-3.5) in human evaluations

Llama 2-Chat employed extensive RLHF alignment work — Meta's paper devoted substantial space to describing their optimization of safety and helpfulness, which was uncommon in open-source "raw base model" releases. Meta had clearly learned from the Sydney incident and other AI safety controversies.

### Impact

Llama 2's commercial license was a tipping point. Before this, discussions about "open-source large models" always carried one question mark: can it be used commercially? Llama 2 answered that question. Thereafter, commercial products based on Llama 2 began to emerge — from enterprise AI assistants to industry-specific custom models. It transformed open-source LLMs from "academic toys" into "commercially viable infrastructure."

### Commentary

From "leak accident" to "voluntary open source," Llama 2 was one of the most successful strategic pivots in large model history. Meta took 145 days to complete a brand reinvention: what was once a stolen model became a model revered as the "flagship of open-source AI."

The business logic behind this was clear to the point of being almost cold-blooded. Zuckerberg did not need to sell API tokens to make money — Meta's revenue came from advertising. Open-sourcing Llama posed zero threat to Meta (it didn't sell AI services) but posed an enormous threat to Google and OpenAI. Every enterprise that deployed Llama was one fewer customer buying a closed-source API. Open source was not charity — open source was competition.

Another angle that often goes unnoticed: Microsoft simultaneously invested in OpenAI (closed-source) and distributed Llama 2 through Azure (open-source). This was a masterful move — whether closed-source or open-source won, Microsoft stood on the winner's side. In July 2023, this was the most elegant risk hedge in the entire large model industry.

## Claude 2: The first commercial model with 100K context

> In July 2023, fitting hundreds of pages of documents into a single conversation was still a sci-fi idea. Anthropic turned it into an API parameter. Claude 2 did not surpass GPT-4 across all benchmarks — but it opened a door: letting models process entire books, entire financial reports, entire codebases, rather than just fragments of a few thousand words.

### Release

**2023-07-11** — Anthropic released **Claude 2**, simultaneously launching the public beta chat website claude.ai (limited to the US/UK). API pricing remained the same as Claude 1.3.[^2]

Claude 2 was a critical step for Anthropic in transitioning from "behind-closed-doors research" to "public commercial availability." The previous Claude 1.x was primarily offered to partners through API, with no public-facing interface. Claude 2 was the first to let ordinary users register an account and converse with Claude — nearly eight months behind ChatGPT, but Anthropic was not in a hurry to compete on timing. It bet on safety alignment and context length.

### 100K context: Fitting an entire book into the prompt

Claude 2's most prominent breakthrough was the **100K token context window** (approximately 75,000 words). For comparison: GPT-4's public context was 8K at the time (the 32K version was limited to select partners), and GPT-3.5 was 4K. Claude 2's context was 12.5 times that of GPT-4's public version.[^3]

This meant users could submit hundreds of pages of documents to Claude for analysis in a single pass — entire financial reports, entire legal codes, entire novels. In a demonstration, Anthropic loaded the full text of *The Great Gatsby* (72K tokens), changed a single line, and Claude identified the modification within 22 seconds.

100K context was not just "longer" — it changed the category of viable use cases. Traditional RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) required splitting documents into fragments and performing vector search, which could lose cross-paragraph reasoning and global structure. By placing the entire document directly in context, the model could build its own holistic understanding. Anthropic explicitly drew this comparison in their blog: "For complex questions, this is likely to be much better than vector search-based approaches."[^3]

### Performance improvements

Claude 2 showed significant improvements over Claude 1.3 across multiple benchmarks:[^2]

| Benchmark | Claude 2 | Claude 1.3 | Improvement |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Bar Exam (multiple choice) | 76.5% | 73.0% | +3.5 |
| Codex HumanEval (coding) | 71.2% | 56.0% | +15.2 |
| GSM8k (grade-school math reasoning) | 88.0% | 85.2% | +2.8 |
| GRE Reading/Writing | Above 90% of test-takers | — | — |
| GRE Quantitative Reasoning | Approximately median level | — | — |

Meanwhile, Anthropic reported that Claude 2 achieved a **harmless response rate twice that of Claude 1.3** in internal safety red-team evaluations — continuing the alignment-first approach built on Claude 1.x's Constitutional AI foundation.[^2]

### Commercial deployment

Claude 2 was Anthropic's first product to achieve large-scale commercial deployment. At launch, it was already publicly partnered with two collaborators:

- **Jasper** (AI content platform): The Engineering VP stated that Claude 2 could "go head-to-head with other state-of-the-art models" in long-text, low-latency scenarios, with a context window 3 times larger than competitors
- **Sourcegraph** (code AI platform): Its AI coding assistant Cody used Claude 2's 100K context to deliver more codebase context, while leveraging its more up-to-date training data (including knowledge of newer frameworks and libraries)

These two partnerships represented Claude 2's two core selling points: long-document understanding (Jasper) and large codebase context (Sourcegraph).

### Survival strategy in GPT-4's shadow

The large model landscape of July 2023: GPT-4 had been out for four months, maintaining a clear lead across virtually all mainstream benchmarks. Claude 2 chose not to confront GPT-4 head-on along its strongest dimensions — instead, it selected two breakthrough points that GPT-4 had not yet addressed:

1. **Context length**: GPT-4's public version only had 8K. If you needed to analyze a 50-page contract or a technical manual, Claude 2 was the only choice
2. **Safety transparency**: Anthropic published model cards, Constitutional AI methodology, and red-team evaluation results, while GPT-4's technical report contained almost no parameters or training details

This "asymmetric competition" established Anthropic's subsequent product philosophy — not to build the strongest model, but to build the most useful one; not to compete with you on IQ, but to compete on how long you can go without things going wrong.

### Commentary

Claude 2 was not the strongest model of 2023, but it was the most important "long context" signal of 2023. Before it, 100K context was a research concept. After it, it became a demand that every model had to answer. In early 2024, Gemini 1.5 Pro arrived with 1M context; GPT-4 Turbo expanded to 128K; and China's Kimi made 2-million-character context its core selling point. The starting line of this race was the first footprint Claude 2 planted in July 2023.

At a deeper level: Claude 2 proved that "asymmetric competition" was viable in the large model market. There was not only one path — head-on surpassing GPT-4. You could choose dimensions where GPT-4 was weak or indifferent, establish a beachhead there, and then gradually expand into other dimensions. Anthropic later narrowed the general capability gap with OpenAI step by step through Claude 3 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, but its starting point was not pursuit — it was flanking.
