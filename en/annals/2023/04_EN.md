# April 2023

## The collective debut of Chinese models: ERNIE Bot, Tongyi Qianwen, and ChatGLM

> Four months after ChatGPT's launch, three Chinese internet giants and a Tsinghua University lab each unveiled their answer in the same month. This was not a coordinated effort, but viewed together, they mapped out the roadmap for Chinese AI over the next three years: three paths — closed-source, open-source, and academic open-source — all departing from the same starting point and heading in entirely different directions.

### Background: The shock of ChatGPT

After ChatGPT's launch in November 2022, internet companies worldwide fell into collective panic. Google declared a "Code Red," and Microsoft swiftly integrated GPT-4 into Bing. But in China, the situation was more complicated — ChatGPT did not support registration from China and offered no Chinese-language optimization. This meant Chinese users could neither use it directly nor expect OpenAI to adapt it for the Chinese market.

This left a window for Chinese AI companies: **build a ChatGPT that Chinese people can use.** The time window was narrow — everyone knew OpenAI would not wait, and Google would not wait. Between March and April 2023, China's mainstream large model products were launched in rapid succession within a single month.

### ERNIE Bot: Baidu's full-stack closed-source route

**2023-03-16** — Baidu held a launch event at its Beijing headquarters, officially releasing **ERNIE Bot** (Wenxin Yiyan). CEO Robin Li personally took the stage for the demonstration. On launch day, Baidu's Hong Kong stock fell 6% — the market expressed disappointment that the demo was pre-recorded rather than live.[^1]

ERNIE Bot was based on Baidu's ERNIE series models (iterated from ERNIE 1.0 in 2019 to ERNIE 3.0/4.0) and was at the time the only system in China with **full-stack self-developed** capabilities spanning chips (Kunlun), framework (PaddlePaddle), model (ERNIE), and application (ERNIE Bot + Baidu Search). This was Baidu's biggest asset — not entirely constrained by GPU export controls, not dependent on open-source frameworks.

Baidu chose the **closed-source + API + product matrix** route, highly similar to OpenAI. After launch, ERNIE Bot was progressively integrated into Baidu's entire product line — Search, Maps, Cloud Storage, and Wenku. The strategy was to use AI to transform Baidu's existing massive user products, rather than building a standalone super-app.

By 2024, ERNIE Bot had been upgraded to version 4.0, reportedly surpassing 200 million users. However, in the developer community, Baidu's closed-source strategy and the closed nature of the PaddlePaddle ecosystem remained persistent points of criticism.

### Tongyi Qianwen: Alibaba's open-source + cloud ecosystem route

**2023-04-11** — Alibaba Cloud released **Tongyi Qianwen** at the Alibaba Cloud Summit in Beijing. CEO Daniel Zhang announced that "all Alibaba products will integrate large models in the future."[^2]

Tongyi Qianwen's positioning differed from ERNIE Bot from the start: it was not one person's assistant, but the **AI foundation for all Alibaba products** — DingTalk, Tmall Genie, Taobao Search, Amap, and Alibaba Cloud, all connected.

The more critical strategic move was revealed in August 2023: Alibaba released the **Qwen** open-source series (7B/14B/72B) — the first large-scale open-source LLM by a Chinese internet giant. Subsequently, Qwen 2 (2024-06), Qwen 2.5 (2024-09), and Qwen 3 (2025-04) continued iterating, covering the full spectrum from 0.5B to 235B under Apache 2.0 license — becoming one of the most complete open-source LLM families worldwide.

Alibaba's route formed a stark contrast with Baidu's:

| | Baidu (ERNIE Bot) | Alibaba (Tongyi Qianwen/Qwen) |
|---|---|---|
| Model strategy | Closed-source | Closed-source + open-source dual track |
| Distribution channels | Own product matrix | Alibaba Cloud + open-source community |
| Target users | Consumer (C-end) + Enterprise (B-end) | Developers + Enterprise + Alibaba ecosystem |
| Underlying framework | PaddlePaddle | Open-source compatible (PyTorch) |
| Positioning | China's ChatGPT alternative | China's AI infrastructure |

Alibaba's dual-track strategy proved extremely effective. The open-source Qwen series accumulated hundreds of millions of downloads on HuggingFace, building a massive developer ecosystem, while the closed-source Tongyi Qianwen served Alibaba's own products and cloud customers. **"Open source for community, closed source for revenue"** — this strategy was later pushed to its extreme by DeepSeek.

### ChatGLM: Zhipu's academic open-source route

**2023-03** — Zhipu AI and Tsinghua University jointly released **ChatGLM-6B**, a Chinese conversational model with only 6B parameters. It could run on a single consumer-grade GPU, was open-source, and quickly gained tens of thousands of stars on GitHub.[^3] ChatGLM2-6B followed in 2023-06, expanding the context window to 32K.

ChatGLM's uniqueness lay in two aspects:

**First, an independent technical route.** Unlike ERNIE Bot and Tongyi Qianwen, ChatGLM was not "a Chinese alternative to ChatGPT" — it had its own independent model architecture: **GLM (General Language Model)**. GLM was proposed in 2022 as an autoregressive blank infilling pre-training framework — simultaneously possessing BERT-style bidirectional understanding and GPT-style unidirectional generation. In an era when the Transformer decoder dominated everything, GLM was a truly independent architectural choice.

**Second, academic origins.** Zhipu AI was spun off from Professor Tang Jie's team at Tsinghua University. This gave ChatGLM a strong flavor of academic open-source from its inception — model weights were public, technical reports were detailed, and community fine-tuning and secondary development were encouraged. This formed a third pole alongside Baidu's "fully closed stack" and Alibaba's "dual-track commercialization."

Zhipu's evolution:
- **2023-03 to 2023-06**: ChatGLM-6B series — academic open-source, community co-development
- **2024-01**: GLM-4 — 128K context, multimodal, All-Tools, competing with GPT-4, entering the commercial stage
- **2026-04**: GLM-5.1 — the core force for domestic substitution after the March 18 Incident, with strong performance on SWE-bench

From academic open-source to commercial product, Zhipu's trajectory traced a path from a Tsinghua laboratory to the first tier of Chinese AI. During the March 18 Incident (2026-03), GLM-5.1 gained an unexpected trust dividend by emerging unscathed from US model distillation accusations.

### The divergence of three paths

In March–April 2023, ERNIE Bot, Tongyi Qianwen, and ChatGLM appeared almost simultaneously. But within just one year, they had arrived at entirely different positions:

- **Baidu** chose the path most resembling OpenAI's — closed-source + strong product binding + full-stack self-development. This path depended on a massive product and traffic matrix, but the centrifugal force from developers was also strong. In 2025–2026, when DeepSeek blasted open the market with MIT-licensed open source, Baidu's closed-source strategy began to come under pressure.
- **Alibaba** chose the most flexible path — dual track. Open-source Qwen captured HuggingFace and the global developer ecosystem, while closed-source Tongyi Qianwen served Alibaba Cloud's enterprise customers. This path was commercially stable and ecologically broad, but its "core model capability" was always considered half a step behind the frontier.
- **Zhipu** chose the most principled path — academically driven with an independent architecture. The uniqueness of the GLM architecture helped Zhipu avoid the label of "following ChatGPT." But the costs of academic origins and scale constraints meant Zhipu remained in the second tier of model capability competition throughout 2023–2024, not truly reaching the front row until GLM-5.1.

### Commentary

The wave of Chinese large model launches in the spring of 2023 was less a technology race and more a **route-selection experiment.**

Baidu chose closed-source full-stack — the heaviest path, and also the most natural instinct of a large company: since I have everything (chips, framework, model, products, users), why give anyone else a share? Alibaba chose dual track — the most pragmatic path; Alibaba Cloud's DNA dictated that it must simultaneously do the most closed and the most open things, because its customers demanded both. Zhipu chose academic open-source — the most idealistic path, and also the most rational choice for the team with the fewest resources: when your compute and data both fall short of the giants, open source is the only way to leverage external forces.

Three years later, each path had its victories and defeats. Alibaba's dual track proved sustainable — walking on two legs of community and revenue. Zhipu's open source proved the value of an independent architecture — being different in itself was a defensive moat. And Baidu's closed-source approach faced the most fundamental challenge in 2026 against DeepSeek V4 and GLM-5.1: when a closed-source model's performance is matched by open-source models, where is its brand premium?

This challenge was not Baidu's alone. OpenAI was answering the same question — it just had a two-year head start.
