# January 2024

## Kimi: The rise of an ultra-long-context Chinese assistant

> A young team with roots from Tsinghua built a Chinese AI assistant that could read a 200,000-character novel in one sitting. While large models worldwide were still racing on benchmark scores, Kimi chose a different path: letting the model read everything you give it.

### Launch and background

Kimi was developed by **Moonshot AI**, founded by Zhilin Yang — a Tsinghua undergraduate and Carnegie Mellon University PhD who studied under Apple's AI lead Ruslan Salakhutdinov. Yang had previously contributed to work on XLNet and Transformer-XL, making him one of the most closely watched researchers in China's large model field.[^1]

Kimi initially launched in October 2023, with **ultra-long context** as its core selling point. By early 2024, its context window had reached approximately 200,000 Chinese characters — far exceeding most conversational AI products on the market. Users could upload entire novels, lengthy reports, or multiple contracts in a single pass, allowing Kimi to perform analysis and Q&A within the full context.

### Why "ultra-long context" was especially important in the Chinese market

Long context was not a technical direction unique to Kimi — Anthropic's Claude was also pushing 100K token context, and Gemini 1.5 Pro shocked the industry with a 1M token window. But Kimi brought this capability to the scenarios Chinese users needed most:

- **Long document processing**: Chinese users dealt with vast numbers of PDF papers, government documents, and enterprise reports, often running to hundreds of pages. Traditional AI could only process them in segments, easily losing cross-paragraph logic. Kimi's "read it all at once" mode was naturally suited to this need.
- **Education and research**: China's postgraduate exam and civil service exam populations were enormous, requiring massive reading of textbooks and past exam papers. Kimi's long context allowed users to upload entire reference books and receive contextually relevant answers to specific questions.
- **Law and contracts**: Contracts and court decisions running to dozens of pages — Kimi could perform cross-referencing without losing the relationships between clauses.

This positioning allowed Kimi to avoid head-on competition with ChatGPT on "chat capability" — it was not here for emotional companionship or creative writing; it was a tool that said "throw your stuff in, and I'll help you digest it."

### Zhilin Yang and Moonshot AI

After completing his PhD in 2019, Yang interned at Google Brain and Meta AI. He founded Moonshot AI in March 2023. The company completed multiple funding rounds within less than a year, and by early 2024 its valuation exceeded $2.5 billion, with investors including Sequoia China, Alibaba, Xiaohongshu, and Meituan.[^2]

Moonshot AI's name derives from Pink Floyd's album *The Dark Side of the Moon* — Yang once explained that it represented his belief about AI: technology always has two sides, and the key is to let humanity see "the other side of the moon," i.e., technology for good.

### The 2-million-character milestone

In March 2024, Kimi announced the expansion of its context window from 200,000 to **2 million Chinese characters**, making it one of the longest-context publicly available models in the world at the time. Users could process hundreds of pages of documents, multiple novels, or complete codebases in a single conversation.[^3]

The immediate effect of this milestone was explosive user growth. Kimi's WeChat mini-program and web platform saw a surge in traffic in March 2024, at one point causing server overload. Moonshot AI urgently scaled up capacity in late March but still could not fully meet demand — Kimi became one of the most closely watched independent brands in the "Chinese AI assistant" category.

### Impact

- Kimi proved that Chinese AI startups could compete through "extreme focus on a single dimension" — without needing to surpass GPT-4 across the board, they only needed to excel sufficiently in one dimension (ultra-long context + deep Chinese understanding) to win users.
- It catalyzed the transformation of "long context" from a technical metric into product value: Gemini's 1M token window was a laboratory number; Kimi's 2 million characters was an experience users would actually use.
- Moonshot AI's fundraising trajectory also validated an investment logic: in the Chinese AI market, founding teams with core training capabilities ("Tsinghua-affiliated") were more favored by capital than purely commercial teams.

By mid-2024, Kimi stood alongside ByteDance's Doubao, Baidu's ERNIE Bot, and Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen in the front rank of Chinese AI assistants — something unimaginable just one year earlier, when ChatGPT had just ignited the market.

### Commentary

Kimi's story is an example of "choosing the right battlefield." In early 2024, the dominant narrative in the large model field was capability parity with GPT-4 — who was closer, who would catch up faster. But Kimi did not take this path. It chose a product positioning distinct from ChatGPT: not general-purpose chat, but an assistant that "can read very long documents."

This choice was smart. Chat capability is a fuzzy standard — users struggle to articulate what "natural conversation" means. But "2 million character context" is a clear, verifiable, immediately actionable number. It allowed Kimi to occupy a distinct position in users' minds: want to read something long? Use Kimi.

Of course, ultra-long context was not without its costs — inference latency, degradation of answer quality at extreme window sizes — all challenges Kimi needed to continuously address. But for a product that only launched in the second half of 2023, establishing a firm foothold by January 2024 already demonstrated that Yang Zhilin's team had found the right path.
