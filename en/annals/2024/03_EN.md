# March 2024

## Claude 3: Anthropic stands at the summit of capability for the first time

> "We no longer need to compare with GPT-4 — Claude 3 Opus has surpassed it on most benchmarks." Behind these words was the moment Anthropic had waited three years for: the safety team that left OpenAI had finally built a model stronger than their former employer.

### Release

**2024-03-04** — Anthropic released the **Claude 3** series in three tiers:[^1]

| Version | Positioning | Capability benchmark | API price (input/output $ per million tokens) |
|------|------|----------|:--:|
| **Opus** | Flagship, strongest | Surpassing GPT-4 | $15 / $75 |
| **Sonnet** | Balanced, suited for most tasks | GPT-4 level, faster and cheaper | $3 / $15 |
| **Haiku** | Lightweight, ultra-fast and low-cost | Approaching GPT-3.5, fast | $0.25 / $1.25 |

This was the first time Anthropic **surpassed GPT-4** on mainstream benchmarks. Opus led or matched across MMLU (86.8% vs GPT-4's 86.4%), HumanEval (84.9% vs 67.0%), and GPQA (scientific reasoning).

Compared to Claude 2, Claude 3's leap lay not only in capability — 200K token context window, support for image input (multimodal visual understanding), and dramatically reduced hallucination rates. Anthropic stated that Opus exhibited "near-human-level performance" on complex reasoning tasks.

### Three-tier differentiation

Claude 3's three-tier pricing represented a major step forward for Anthropic in commercialization:

- **Opus** was the "money is no object" option — most expensive, but strongest. For scenarios demanding extreme accuracy: medical diagnosis assistance, legal document analysis, complex code review
- **Sonnet** was "most people's default choice" — moderately priced, 99% of people did not need Opus's power
- **Haiku** was "embedded AI" — extremely fast and cheap, suited for real-time customer service, content moderation, and simple text classification tasks

This "three sizes, three price points, one brand" strategy later proved to be Anthropic's most effective weapon against OpenAI. OpenAI's response — launching o1-mini and GPT-4o-mini — came six months later.

### Addition of visual capabilities

Claude 3 introduced support for image input for the first time — users could upload photos, charts, and PDF scans to Claude, and the model could understand visual content and respond. This was not the GPT-4V-style "can look at images" — it treated visual understanding as a **default capability** of the model, without needing to switch between "vision" and "text" modes.

This was important for Anthropic's customers — many enterprise users' core scenarios involved "upload a file and let AI analyze it" rather than "conversational chat." Claude 3's optimization for this scenario was more thorough than GPT-4's.

### Impact

Claude 3's release marked a new phase in large model competition: **no longer OpenAI alone at the top, but a three-way balance** — GPT-4 still had the best multimodality and brand, Claude 3 had safety and honesty differentiation, and Gemini 1.5 had ultra-long context and the Google ecosystem. For the first time, users had genuine choice — not because any one model "won," but because each had its own strengths.

For Anthropic itself, Claude 3 was a milestone: it was finally no longer viewed as "the safety-sentiment project of an OpenAI exodus team," but as a **product with genuine competitive capability.**

### Commentary

Claude 3's release marked a subtle historical inflection point: it arrived even earlier than what was written in the Annals of OpenAI about "OpenAI facing an identity crisis in 2024."

The significance of Claude 3 Opus surpassing GPT-4 on benchmarks was not "Claude is stronger" — it was "the closed-source frontier is no longer a monopoly." This produced second-order effects on the entire ecosystem: multi-vendor strategies became viable options for enterprises, benchmark evaluations shifted from "watching whether GPT-4 ticked up again" to "seeing who won this round" — a decentralized competitive landscape began to take shape.

But what deserved more attention was Anthropic's three-tier pricing strategy. It essentially converted the brand asset of "safety" into price discrimination: the safest model sold at the highest price (Opus), the fastest model sold at the cheapest price (Haiku), and the middle tier (Sonnet) was most people's practical choice. The elegance of this three-tier structure lay in what it did *not* compete on — it didn't compare with GPT-4 on who was more creative, who could write better poetry, who was better at flirting. It competed on "choose me because I'm honest." In a market plagued by hallucination problems, "honesty" itself was a product attribute.

However, Claude 3's success also planted a question: when competitors' models also become "safer," where is Anthropic's moat? This question was partially answered by Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Artifacts — but that is another story.
