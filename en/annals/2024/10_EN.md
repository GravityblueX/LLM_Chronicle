# October 2024

## October 8

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced that the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics would be awarded to John J. Hopfield of Princeton University (USA) and Geoffrey E. Hinton of the University of Toronto (Canada) for their foundational discoveries and inventions in enabling machine learning with artificial neural networks. On the day of the award, Hinton told interviewers he "never expected to win the Physics Prize" since his field was computer science; he also expressed concern about the rapid development of AI technology and called on society to take AI safety seriously. The Hopfield network (1982) and the Boltzmann machine (1985) were the core contributions behind the award — the former used spin models from physics to construct associative memory networks, while the latter introduced statistical mechanics into machine learning; together, they laid the theoretical foundations of deep learning. This was the first time in Nobel Prize history that the Physics Prize was awarded to researchers whose core work was in computational science, sparking widespread debate in academia about "the boundaries of physics."[^1][^2]

## October 22

Anthropic released an upgraded version of Claude 3.5 Sonnet (later called "Claude 3.5 Sonnet New"), significantly improving coding capability (SWE-bench Verified 49.0%→53.0%) and tool use while maintaining the same inference speed as its predecessor. The most eye-catching aspect of this update was the simultaneous public beta release of the "computer use" feature — Claude could operate desktop environments following natural language instructions, including moving the mouse cursor, clicking buttons, and typing text. The feature was exposed through three new APIs: computer operations (screenshot → coordinate clicks/keyboard input), text editor (file read/write and command execution), and tool calling (structured interaction). Anthropic acknowledged in its announcement that the feature was still "clunky and error-prone," but had practical utility as a first-generation product. Claude 3.5 Haiku, also released on the same day, replaced Claude 3 Haiku at lower pricing and faster speeds, while achieving performance comparable to the previous generation's flagship, Claude 3 Opus.[^3][^4][^5]

## Other events this month

- On October 17, xAI opened the Grok API to developers (previously Grok was limited to internal use on the X platform), offering the `grok-beta` model with pricing of $5 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, along with function calling and system prompt capabilities — marking Musk's AI business beginning to expand from consumer to enterprise.[^6][^7]
