# August 2024

## FLUX.1: The true successor to SD3

> Stability AI's core R&D team departed en masse, founded a new company, and released a new model. FLUX.1 carried none of SD3's legal baggage and none of its anatomical defects. It was the "pick up the torch and keep running" moment for the Stable Diffusion lineage, after licensing controversies, CEO resignation, and corporate acquisition.

### Release

**2024-08-01** — Black Forest Labs released **FLUX.1**. The core team — Robin Rombach et al. — were precisely the core researchers behind Stable Diffusion, who departed after Stability AI's financial and management collapse.[^1]

FLUX.1 employed a **DiT (Diffusion Transformer)** + **Flow Matching** architecture combination, following the same technical trajectory as SD3 — but with cleaner execution. Key improvements:

- **Human anatomy**: SD3 was heavily criticized by the community for anatomical defects (incorrect finger counts, limb distortions, bizarre joint rendering). FLUX.1 achieved fundamental improvements in human figure generation quality
- **Text rendering**: FLUX.1's text generation capability far surpassed any prior open-source model — accurately generating signs, posters, and logos with specified text
- **Open-source licensing**: FLUX.1-dev was released under a non-commercial license; FLUX.1-schnell (distilled version) under Apache 2.0[^1]

After release, FLUX.1 rapidly became the new standard in the open-source text-to-image community — ComfyUI workflows migrated en masse, and Civitai model publication volume surged.

### The endgame of the SD3 lineage

FLUX.1's release marked a "baton handoff" in the Stable Diffusion story:

| Stage | Model | Date | Status |
|------|------|------|------|
| Beginning | SD 1.4/1.5 | 2022-08 | Open-source revolution |
| Growth | SDXL | 2023-07 | Resolution upgrade |
| Pivot | SD3 | 2024-02 to 2024-06 | Architectural shift to DiT, but licensing controversy + deployment issues |
| Handoff | FLUX.1 | 2024-08 | Same team, same trajectory, better execution |

Stability AI was acquired at the end of 2024. But its technical legacy — the open-source text-to-image trajectory — continued to evolve under FLUX.1's stewardship. This was a rare pattern in large model history: **the technology was not abandoned; the team carried it out and continued iterating under a new banner.**

### Impact

FLUX.1's value operated on two levels: technically, it proved that the DiT + Flow Matching approach outperformed traditional UNet diffusion for image generation; industrially, it proved that the "team exodus → re-founding → continued iteration" model was viable in the large model field — technology follows people, not companies.

### Commentary

FLUX.1 is a rare footnote of "technology immortal" in large model history. Stability AI nearly disintegrated in 2024 — the CEO resigned, finances collapsed, SD3's licensing controversy and product defects drove the community away — yet the core R&D team of the SD series carried the same technical direction and cleaner execution forward under the Black Forest Labs banner. The company and brand died, but the technical trajectory and the people survived.

This is uncommon in the AI industry. The more typical pattern in large model history is either "company alive, team intact, technical trajectory intact" or "company dead, team disbanded, technical trajectory severed." FLUX.1 demonstrated a new possibility — "a company can die, but a technical trajectory can continue growing in a new container." This was especially important for the open ecosystem: it meant technology was no longer held hostage by corporate fate.

An interesting counterpoint is OpenAI. If OpenAI's core team departed en masse and re-implemented the GPT series' technical trajectory — which is entirely feasible in engineering — GPT as a technical lineage would continue, but OpenAI as a company would instantly lose its competitive edge. This is the deeper logic FLUX.1 revealed: when a model's core technology is sufficiently transparent and the open-source community's replication capability is sufficiently strong, a company's moat is not its model — it is its talent.
