# February 2024

## Sora: When video transforms from pixels to tokens

> A single prompt, a 60-second video. Sora was not publicly available, had no API, had no product — but the video clips it released turned "AI video generation" from a blurry lab demo into something disturbingly realistic. Everyone knew the ChatGPT moment of video generation was coming; they just didn't expect it so soon.

### Release

**2024-02-15** — OpenAI released **Sora**, a text-to-video model based on the **DiT** (Diffusion Transformer) architecture. It could generate videos up to 60 seconds in 1080p, with visual consistency and object persistence far surpassing the prior Runway Gen-2 and Pika.[^1]

Sora's core technology represented video as "spacetime patches" — analogous to how ViT splits images into patches, Sora split video into three-dimensional spatiotemporal blocks, then used DiT (a combination of diffusion models and Transformers) to generate them in latent space. This gave Sora inherent **3D consistency** for objects — the same object maintained its shape and texture after moving, being occluded, or rotating.

OpenAI's demo videos were stunning: a stylish woman walking through neon-lit streets in Tokyo on a rainy night, puddle reflections rippling with her footsteps, passersby naturally occluding her as they crossed in front of the camera — then she calmly emerged from behind the occlusion, with clothing, hairstyle, and gait all perfectly consistent. This was not achievable through "generate each frame independently and stitch them together." Sora had developed a rough but disturbing "understanding" of the physical world.

### Undisclosed deployment

Sora was not opened to the public as expected after its announcement. OpenAI cited reasons including: safety evaluations, preventing deepfake abuse, excessive computational cost, and the need to maintain distance from the 2024 US presidential election. But external observers widely believed inference cost was the real reason — the computational requirements for generating 60 seconds of high-quality video far exceeded those for text and image generation. Without significant optimization, Sora was not ready for large-scale API deployment.

Sora was not opened to select creators for testing until late 2024. By early 2025, it still had not become a product for everyone like ChatGPT. But Sora's announcement alone had already changed the industry's trajectory — **just as GPT-3's release made everyone aware of the direction of scaling, Sora's release made everyone aware that the DiT approach to video generation was the right one.**

### Impact

Sora was the "GPT-3 moment" of text-to-video generation. After its release, video generation model R&D worldwide pivoted entirely to the DiT route. The previous mainstream approaches (UNet-based diffusion models + temporal attention) were rapidly abandoned. China's Kuaishou Kling (2024-06), ByteDance Jimeng, Runway Gen-3, and other subsequent entrants all adopted DiT or DiT-like architectures.

Sora also represented OpenAI's furthest foray into the "announce but don't deliver" strategy. From its February 2024 announcement to actual availability in mid-2025, more than a year elapsed. This "technology preview" style of release generated increasing community frustration — "If you're not going to let me use it, why show it to me?"

## Gemini 1.5 Pro: Fitting *War and Peace* into context

> One million tokens — eight times more than GPT-4's 128K, ten times more than Claude's 100K. Gemini 1.5 Pro was not competing with GPT-4 on benchmarks — it was competing with everyone on "how much your context can hold." In that contest, it won.

### Release

**Same day, 2024-02-15** (the same day as Sora — Google tried to counter OpenAI's nuclear weapon with its own) — Google DeepMind released **Gemini 1.5 Pro**, with its core selling point being a **1M token context window**.[^2]

What did 1M tokens mean? You could input all at once:
- The entirety of *War and Peace* (approximately 560K tokens)
- A complete movie screenplay
- The entire source code of a moderately sized codebase
- Hours of audio transcription

In the "Needle In A Haystack" test — hiding a piece of information somewhere in a long text and testing whether the model can find it — Gemini 1.5 Pro achieved 99% accuracy in a 1M token context.

The technical foundation was the **MoE (Mixture of Experts) architecture** — Gemini 1.5 Pro maintained capabilities close to 1.0 Ultra while dramatically reducing inference costs. It also featured specialized optimizations to the attention mechanism for long contexts, making "ultra-long context" not just a parameter that was supported, but truly usable.

### Impact

Gemini 1.5 Pro's 1M token context window redefined the standard for "ultra-long context." Thereafter, virtually every AI company was compelled to compete on long context as a core capability. GPT-4 Turbo's 128K was still astonishing at the end of 2023; by the first half of 2024, it was no longer enough. Google opened a new battlefield — "who remembers more" — and on that battlefield, it built a temporarily unassailable moat using its TPU and Google-grade infrastructure.

### Commentary

Ultra-long context was an "asymmetric war." Google chose not to fight OpenAI head-on on reasoning, coding, and multimodal interaction — the territories GPT-4 had already claimed — and instead established a new metric where it could reign supreme.

The cleverness of this choice lay in the fact that the hardware barrier for long context was much higher than for multimodality or reasoning. GPT-4's multimodal capability could be matched by Claude 3 — Anthropic simply invested a few more months in computer vision training. But truly usable 1M token context required more than algorithms — it required TPU v5p, Google's proprietary inference infrastructure, and the engineering capability to "throw the entirety of *War and Peace* into the model" without it crashing. All three were unique to Google.

Gemini 1.5 Pro ultimately could not make Google surpass GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet in overall capability — but it established a lead of over one year on the specific metric of long context. This strategy of "winning on a track the opponent doesn't play on" was later widely replicated by other challengers: Mistral won on open-source efficiency, DeepSeek won on API pricing, Anthropic won on safety characterization. Large model competition shifted from "who is strongest" to "who is strongest on their own track" — and Gemini 1.5 Pro in February 2024 was the first signal of this fragmentation.
