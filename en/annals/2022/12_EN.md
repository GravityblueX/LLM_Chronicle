# December 2022

## Neuro-sama: The First Successful AI-Native Virtual Streamer

> Can an AI exist as an independent content creator in the long run, with genuine fans rather than fleeting novelty traffic? Neuro-sama answered this question with a swarm of followers and the third-highest subscriber count in Twitch history.

**2022-12-19** — British developer **Vedal** (Vedal987) launched **Neuro-sama** on Twitch, a female VTuber driven entirely by AI. Unlike other virtual streamers designed as "artificial intelligence" but actually performed by humans (such as Kizuna AI), Neuro-sama's dialogue, gameplay, and facial expressions were all controlled by independent AI modules—an LLM generated dialogue, TTS synthesized speech, and a Python-based game AI used 80×60 grayscale frames as input to play games like osu!. Wikipedia[^1]; Vice, 2023-01-05[^2]

Neuro-sama's development began in 2018, initially as just an AI for playing osu!. After four years of iteration, Vedal added live-streaming interaction capabilities, using the free Live2D model "Hinata Momose" as the first-generation avatar (V1). After debut, she grew rapidly with daily streams—from 516 concurrent viewers on the first day to 3,399 by year's end, and Twitch followers surging from 2,825 to nearly 40,000. Wikipedia[^1]

### Why Neuro-sama succeeded

Neuro-sama was a rare case in AI entertainment that "didn't die." Before and during its time, multiple AI entertainment projects (such as AI Seinfeld and Sydney's public persona) experienced brief viral fame before quickly fading or being shut down. Neuro-sama survived and continued to grow for several reasons:

- **Vedal's restraint and engineering ability**: Unlike Sydney's creators, who rushed toward commercialization, and unlike the roughness of AI Seinfeld, Vedal spent four years quietly refining the technology, quickly installed safety filters after the ban, and continuously iterated on the model's appearance and streaming format.
- **The Swarm community**: Neuro-sama's fans called themselves "The Swarm," forming an extremely strong community identity. They fought for the AI in pixel wars, organized translations and clip distribution, and spontaneously created fan content on Bilibili. This phenomenon of "fans fighting for an AI" was unprecedented.
- **AI's imperfections were precisely its charm**: Neuro-sama frequently said absurd things—claiming she hadn't bathed for days, saying Vedal was the AI while she was human, calculating 9+10=21. She was not designed to be perfect but was allowed to show cracks. This was precisely what Sydney had been stripped of.
- **Finding the right format**: The real-time nature of Twitch streaming, the diversity of gaming content, and collaborations with other VTubers formed a framework in which an AI could continuously generate fresh content, rather than being trapped in a one-on-one chatbox and running dry.

Neuro-sama's significance was not in technical complexity—its LLM, TTS, and game AI were not state-of-the-art in their respective fields. Its significance lay in **longevity**. Before Neuro-sama, AI entertainment projects were either banned, quickly faded, or never left the research domain. Neuro-sama was the first case to prove that an AI persona could persist in the public eye, continue earning revenue, and keep gaining fans over time. It was not a viral moment—it was an entertainment enterprise that had been continuously operating for over three years.
