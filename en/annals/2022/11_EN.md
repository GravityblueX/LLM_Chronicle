# November 2022

## ChatGPT: The "iPhone Moment" of LLMs

> One million users in five days, one hundred million in two months. ChatGPT was not the most powerful model—it used GPT-3.5, something that had been running in the API for months. But put it in a chatbox, add RLHF alignment, and the entire world changed.

### Release

**2022-11-30** — OpenAI released **ChatGPT**, a conversational AI product based on GPT-3.5. Free to use, no API key required—just open the webpage and start chatting.[^1]

The model behind ChatGPT was **GPT-3.5**—GPT-3 after instruction fine-tuning (using InstructGPT's methodology) and RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback). It had been available to developers via API since early 2022 (under the name `text-davinci-003`), but ChatGPT was the first attempt to package it as a "chat product."

ChatGPT's growth rate was unprecedented:[^2]

| Milestone | Date | Days |
|-----------|------|------|
| 1 million users | 2022-12-05 | **5 days** |
| 100 million monthly active users | 2023-01-31 | **2 months** |

For comparison: TikTok took 9 months to reach 100 million users, and Instagram took 2.5 years. ChatGPT set the fastest record for internet product growth.

### Why it was the "iPhone moment"

The "iPhone moment" metaphor has been used repeatedly since, but it fits ChatGPT precisely. The reason was not that the technology was particularly new:

- **Before**: Large language models were researchers' toys, developers' APIs, and internal tools at a handful of companies. Ordinary people did not know what a "language model" was, nor did they care.
- **After**: Everyone could converse with an AI—ask it for recipes, have it write emails, debate philosophy with it. The large language model transformed from a technical concept into a popular verb—"Let me ask ChatGPT."

The core of ChatGPT's success was not GPT-3.5's capability (it had existed since early 2022) but the transformation of product form—turning an API into a chatbox. No need to select a model version, no need to adjust temperature and top_p, no need to understand token billing. Open the webpage, type, press Enter. That was all. This simplicity transformed AI from something requiring technical knowledge into something everyone carried in their pocket.

Another key factor was **the sense of alignment brought by RLHF**. ChatGPT would acknowledge mistakes, refuse inappropriate requests, and explain its own limitations—these behaviors were not inherent to GPT-3 but were trained through human feedback as described in the InstructGPT paper.[^3] For the first time, users felt: "This AI is having a conversation with me, not auto-completing my sentences."

### Sam Altman's "calculator" metaphor

On the day ChatGPT launched, Sam Altman tweeted: "ChatGPT is incredibly limited, but good enough at some things to create a misleading impression of greatness. it's a mistake to be relying on it for anything important right now. it's a preview of progress; we have lots of work to do on robustness and truthfulness."

It was a strange launch tweet—a CEO saying his own product had "serious limitations," "don't rely on it for anything important," and "it's just a preview of progress." But this was OpenAI's strategy at the time: release an imperfect product first, let users discover its capabilities and limitations, then iterate rapidly. This was later summarized as "ship early, iterate publicly."

He also posted another tweet that became widely cited: "ChatGPT is like a calculator. Calculators didn't replace mathematicians, but every mathematician has a calculator. AI won't replace writers, but every writer will have an AI."

### Technical lineage

ChatGPT did not appear out of thin air. Its direct predecessors were:

- **GPT-3** (2020-06): Demonstrated few-shot capabilities, but generation quality was unstable and it could be easily induced into producing harmful content
- **InstructGPT** (2022-01): Used RLHF to align GPT-3 toward "following instructions, being helpful, and being harmless." ChatGPT was essentially the conversational version of InstructGPT
- **GPT-3.5 series**: `code-davinci-002` (code-enhanced version) and `text-davinci-003` (instruction-tuned version) had been running in the API for months before ChatGPT's release

The specific RLHF pipeline: human annotators rank multiple model-generated responses → a reward model is trained to predict human preferences → PPO reinforcement learning optimizes the language model to maximize reward. The paper describing this method was published in March 2022, but it was not until ChatGPT that the world truly felt what "alignment" meant.[^3]

### Shockwaves

In the months following ChatGPT's release, the entire tech industry experienced unprecedented upheaval:

- **Google issued a "Code Red"**: According to *The New York Times*, after ChatGPT's release, Google's management declared a "Code Red"—fearing that ChatGPT threatened Google's core search business. This directly drove the hasty release of Bard (2023-03-21)
- **Education system panic and adaptation**: From "banning ChatGPT entirely" to "teaching students how to use AI tools properly," the global education system underwent a rapid attitude shift. The New York City Department of Education banned ChatGPT in January 2023 and reversed the ban in May
- **Explosion of AI writing tools**: AI writing tools like Jasper, Copy.ai, and Writer experienced explosive growth after ChatGPT's release. VC funding poured frantically into "GPT wrapper" startups
- **The "wrapper" debate**: Numerous startups built on the OpenAI API were questioned as "just ChatGPT wrappers"—without their own models or technical moats. This issue received brutal validation during the March 2024 API incident: when the API was cut off, "wrapper" companies went to zero overnight
- **Microsoft's billion-dollar bet**: On 2023-01-23, Microsoft announced a "multi-year, multi-billion-dollar" new investment in OpenAI—the $1 billion invested in 2019 was just the beginning. ChatGPT's success gave Microsoft enormous confidence

### Subsequent iterations

- **2023-02-01**: ChatGPT Plus subscription launched ($20/month), offering priority access and faster response times
- **2023-03-14**: GPT-4 released, ChatGPT Plus users could select GPT-4 mode
- **2023-05**: ChatGPT mobile app launched
- **2023-09**: ChatGPT added multimodal support (image input) and web browsing
- **2023-11**: GPTs (custom GPTs) and GPT Store released—allowing users to create customized ChatGPT variants
- **2024-05**: GPT-4o launched, available to free users, supporting real-time voice conversation

### Impact

ChatGPT was not the most powerful model in the history of large language models, but it was **the most important product**. It transformed large language models from "what this technology can do" into "what I can do with this technology." From November 30, 2022, "AI" was no longer just a research field—it became a conversation everyone could participate in. The subsequent GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, Llama—they all ran on the track that ChatGPT opened.

More importantly, ChatGPT proved a commercial hypothesis: **"AI conversation" is an independent product category, not an add-on feature.** Before ChatGPT, AI assistants were appendages of search (Google Assistant), phone features (Siri), and IoT ornaments (Alexa). After ChatGPT, "chatting with AI" became a purpose in itself.

### Commentary

The key to ChatGPT's success lay not in the model itself—GPT-3.5 had already existed for months—but in the product form. Turning an LLM from an API into a chatbox appeared to be a trivial UX change, but it accomplished two critical things:

First, **it lowered the "comprehension barrier."** There was no need to know what "language model" meant, no need to understand the difference between "inference" and "training," no need to adjust parameters, select endpoints, or count tokens. Chatting is humanity's most natural mode of interaction—three-year-olds can chat, eighty-year-olds can chat. Putting AI in a chatbox meant putting AI into humanity's oldest interface.

Second, **it demonstrated the value of "alignment."** ChatGPT would decline to answer inappropriate questions, admit when it did not know something, and apologize when it made mistakes—these behaviors made users feel the AI was "safe." And "feeling safe" is a necessary prerequisite for the general public to accept a new technology. RLHF was to ChatGPT what seatbelts were to automobiles—not for performance, but for trust.

Ironically, ChatGPT's success also exposed OpenAI's awkwardness: the company's name contains "Open," yet ChatGPT was a thoroughly closed-source product—no public model weights, no public training data, no public technical details. This tension of "practicing closed-source in the name of open-source" continued to ferment in subsequent years—and directly catalyzed the Llama open-source movement and DeepSeek's MIT-licensed open-source counterstrike.
