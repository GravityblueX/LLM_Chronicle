# October 2022

## Flan-T5: A Large-Scale Validation of Instruction Fine-Tuning

> OpenAI released InstructGPT in early 2022, proving that RLHF could make models more obedient. Google answered the same question in October 2022 using a different path: if thousands of NLP tasks are converted into natural language instructions for training, can the model generalize to entirely unseen tasks? The answer was yes—and it performed far better than larger models that had not been instruction-tuned.

### Release

**2022-10-20** — Google Research submitted the paper *"Scaling Instruction-Finetuned Language Models"* and publicly released the **Flan-T5** series of model weights (based on the T5 architecture, spanning multiple scales from small to XXL).[^1]

The core proposition of this work was: can instruction fine-tuning be scaled up? The Google team conducted systematic experiments in three directions—expanding the number of tasks (ultimately using 1,836 NLP tasks), expanding model scale, and incorporating chain-of-thought data.

### Key results

The most critical data point: after instruction fine-tuning on 1,836 tasks, Flan-PaLM 540B achieved **75.2% on MMLU (5-shot)**—an average improvement of **9.4 percentage points** over the unfine-tuned PaLM 540B. Notably, this gain came not from a larger model but from a better training methodology.[^1]

An even more surprising result: Flan-T5-XXL (11B parameters) in few-shot settings **outperformed PaLM 62B**—a model with less than one-fifth the parameters but instruction-tuned, defeating an unfine-tuned model with five times more parameters. This demonstrated that instruction fine-tuning was not merely a nice-to-have enhancement but a competitive alternative to "scaling up parameters."[^1]

### Why it mattered

Flan-T5's significance lay not in raw model capability—after ChatGPT's release in late 2022, it was quickly eclipsed in public perception. But in the engineering evolution of large language models, it left an important methodological contribution:

1. **Instruction fine-tuning is general-purpose**: Unifying hundreds or thousands of NLP tasks into the format of "input an instruction → output a text response" enabled models to learn the meta-ability of "understanding instructions" rather than memorizing each specific command.
2. **Chain-of-thought data helps**: The paper explicitly tested the effect of including chain-of-thought reasoning examples in fine-tuning data, finding significant performance improvements on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning (such as arithmetic and commonsense reasoning). This was a full step ahead of ChatGPT and subsequent reasoning models.
3. **Model weights were publicly released**: Flan-T5's weights were published on GitHub and HuggingFace, enabling the research community to reproduce and build upon them. In the context of late 2022, when InstructGPT provided only papers without weights, Flan-T5 was the strongest publicly available instruction-tuned model at the time.[^2]

From InstructGPT (2022-01) to Flan-T5 (2022-10) to ChatGPT (2022-11), 2022 was the starting point of the technical trajectory of "making models more obedient." Flan-T5 was Google's contribution to this trajectory—it was not as sensational as ChatGPT, but its systematic experiments established the methodological foundation for subsequent instruction fine-tuning.

### Commentary

Flan-T5 was the kind of work that "was underestimated at the time but frequently cited later." Instruction fine-tuning on 1,836 tasks—this number is not large by today's standards, but in 2022 it meant that Google's research team was already systematically thinking about "whether models could learn cross-task generalization from a large volume of diverse instructions."

Its historical position sits in a delicate time window: preceded by InstructGPT (OpenAI) and followed by ChatGPT (OpenAI). Both came from the same company; Flan-T5 came from Google. In this narrative, Google did the research right but left the productization opportunity to OpenAI. This was not the first time, nor would it be the last. But as a piece of research, Flan-T5 transformed "instruction fine-tuning" from an idea into a repeatable experimental methodology—and for that alone, it deserves an entry in this chronicle.
