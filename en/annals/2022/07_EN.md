# July 2022

## BLOOM: The Open-Source Multilingual Large Model Born from a Thousand-Person Collaboration

> Over 1,000 researchers, 60+ countries, a French government supercomputer, more than a year of collaboration—the BigScience collective delivered BLOOM: 176 billion parameters, 46 natural languages, 13 programming languages. It was not the most powerful large language model, but it answered a question that no one had truly addressed before—can academia and the open-source community independently train a frontier-scale large model?

**2022-07-12** — The BigScience collective officially released **BLOOM** (BigScience Large Open-science Open-access Multilingual Language Model) with 176B parameters, making it the largest open-source multilingual language model at the time.[^1] Model weights were made available for download via the HuggingFace Hub, accompanied by a complete Model Card and data documentation.

BLOOM was led by Hugging Face, with support from the French National Center for Scientific Research (CNRS), the French National Computing Center (GENCI), and multiple European academic institutions.[^2] Planning for the project began in early 2021, bringing together over 1,000 researchers from more than 60 countries and regions—hailing from disciplines including natural language processing, machine learning, linguistics, law, ethics, and sociology.

### Model specifications

| Specification | Value |
|---------------|-------|
| Parameters | 176B |
| Architecture | Decoder-only Transformer (same family as GPT-3) |
| Training data | ROOTS corpus, 46 natural languages + 13 programming languages, approximately 366B tokens |
| Training hardware | 384× A100 80GB GPUs (Jean Zay supercomputer, French IDRIS) |
| Training duration | Approximately 3.5 months (March–July 2022) |
| License | RAIL (Responsible AI License) |

The **ROOTS corpus** was the core of BLOOM's training data, built from scratch by the BigScience data working group.[^3] It was not simply scraped from the internet; instead, it consciously balanced the representation of different languages. Beyond English and French, it covered Spanish, Arabic, Chinese, Vietnamese, Yoruba, Swahili, and dozens of other languages, many of which were low-resource languages that had almost never been seriously represented in large model training before.

### Why it mattered

When BLOOM was released, the technical community immediately noted that its performance on English benchmarks fell short of PaLM-540B (2022-04) and even the earlier Chinchilla-70B (2022-03).[^1] Although the 176B parameter count was comparable to GPT-3, its training efficiency and data proportions had already fallen behind the latest understanding of 2022.

But BLOOM's value was not on the benchmark leaderboard. Its significance lay in three things:

**First, it proved that non-profit, non-corporate collaboration could train frontier-scale models.** Over 1,000 researchers, a government supercomputer, more than a year of coordination—this was not a replicable business model, but it was a replicable organizational model. BLOOM's project organization drew on the model of large-scale physics experiments (such as CERN): no single PI or company dominated; instead, work was divided among working groups—data, ethics, model, and governance each had dedicated teams.[^2]

**Second, it was the first serious open-source multilingual large model.** With coverage of 46 natural languages and 13 programming languages, it became the first truly usable foundation model for non-English communities studying large language models.

**Third, it pioneered the "open science + open model" release paradigm.** It released not only weights but also data construction methodology, ethics review processes, and governance decision records. This level of transparency was unique among large model releases at the time.

### The RAIL License

BLOOM adopted the RAIL License (Responsible AI License), a pioneering attempt at "responsible openness."[^4] Unlike traditional open-source licenses such as MIT or Apache 2.0, RAIL permitted downloading and using model weights while attaching usage restrictions—prohibiting the use of the model for generating disinformation, surveillance systems, weapons development, and other scenarios.

This sparked heated debate in the open-source community: does a license with attached usage restrictions count as "truly open source"? This debate was a rehearsal for the much larger controversy in 2024 surrounding the OSI's Open Source AI Definition. The RAIL License ultimately became a reference point for many subsequent model license designs—the Llama 2 Community License, Mistral's Apache 2.0, and DeepSeek's custom licenses all sought different balances between "degree of openness" and "usage restrictions."

### Aftermath

After BLOOM's release, the HuggingFace ecosystem expanded rapidly. In May 2022, HuggingFace completed a $100 million Series C funding round (at a $2 billion valuation), and in August 2023, it completed another round at a $4.5 billion valuation.[^5] The BLOOM project was a critical milestone in HuggingFace's evolution from "model hosting platform" to "open-source AI infrastructure provider."

By 2024–2025, BLOOM as a "directly usable model" had been surpassed by successors such as Llama 2/3, Qwen, and Mistral—these models significantly outperformed BLOOM in both English and multilingual performance, with lower training costs and easier deployment. But BLOOM's legacy as an "organizational model" remains alive. It occupies a special position on the timeline of the large model open-source movement: it was the most important open-source large model event before the LLaMA leak, and it served as the bridge between the "GPT-3 era, where only companies could train models" and the "Llama era, where communities can fine-tune everything."

### Commentary

BLOOM's benchmark scores were not the strongest even at the time of release. Its significance lay in answering a question that almost no one dared to confront directly in 2021: can frontier-scale large models be independently trained by openly collaborative academic groups?

The answer was yes. BLOOM demonstrated a CERN-like possibility: large model training does not necessarily require corporate organizational structures, commercial return expectations, and closed-door development. Although this model was not subsequently replicated at scale (after LLaMA, the open-source movement shifted toward "companies open-source weights, communities fine-tune"), BLOOM's pioneering status is irreplaceable.

In the broader context of the open-source movement, BLOOM was the "manifesto," LLaMA was the "uprising," and Llama 2 was the "establishment." BLOOM spoke first—though the reverberations of that voice would not truly become a tsunami until the LLaMA leak a year later.
