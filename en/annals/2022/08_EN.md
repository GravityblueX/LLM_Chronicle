# August 2022

## Stable Diffusion: The "Cambrian Explosion" of Open-Source Text-to-Image Generation

> A single open-source model gave rise to tens of thousands of LoRAs, ControlNets, the Civitai community, and the first year of AI painting—the diffusion model was no longer just a formula in research papers, but a brush in everyone's computer.

**2022-08-22** — Stability AI officially released **Stable Diffusion** (SD), an open-source text-to-image model based on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM). Developed by the CompVis team at the University of Munich (Robin Rombach et al.) with compute support from Stability AI, the model was trained on the LAION-5B dataset and could run on consumer-grade GPUs (as low as 4GB VRAM)—something that closed-source models like DALL·E 2 and MidJourney could not achieve at the time. Stability AI official blog, 2022-08-22[^1]; Wikipedia[^2]

SD's core innovation lay not in the algorithm (the LDM architecture was previously published by CompVis at CVPR 2022) but in **engineering and ecosystem**: it transformed the diffusion model from a concept in papers into a tool that could run on a 4GB graphics card. It also adopted a permissive open-source license (CreativeML Open RAIL-M), allowing commercial use and secondary development.

### Version history

| Version | Date | Key changes |
|---------|------|-------------|
| SD 1.4/1.5 | 2022-08/10 | First widely adopted versions, 512×512 resolution. Spawned the earliest LoRA/Civitai ecosystem[^3] |
| SD 2.0/2.1 | 2022-11 | Upgraded to 768×768, but removed large amounts of NSFW and celebrity data, triggering strong community backlash |
| SDXL 1.0 | 2023-07 | 1024×1024 native resolution, dual text encoders, significant improvements in composition and detail |
| SD3 | 2024-02–06 | Architecture shifted to DiT (Diffusion Transformer, MMDiT), targeting OpenAI Sora's technical approach. However, the Medium version sparked community dissatisfaction due to licensing controversies and anatomical flaws in human figures[^5] |
| FLUX.1 | 2024-08 | Former Stability AI core team members (Robin Rombach et al.) left to found Black Forest Labs, releasing FLUX.1 with DiT architecture + flow matching, regarded as the true successor to the SD3 lineage |

### Ecosystem impact

The open-sourcing of Stable Diffusion triggered a "Cambrian explosion" in AI image generation:

- **LoRA fine-tuning**: Low-rank adaptation technology was rapidly integrated into the SD ecosystem. Users could train custom style or character models with just a few dozen images and a consumer-grade GPU. To date, hundreds of thousands of public LoRA models have been uploaded to Civitai.
- **ControlNet** (2023-02): Lvmin Zhang's ControlNet provided SD with precise spatial control capabilities—pose, line art, depth maps, edge detection—transforming AI art from "gacha pulling" into "controllable creation."
- **Community and platforms**: Civitai (model sharing), HuggingFace (model hosting), and Stable Diffusion WebUI (the user-friendly interface developed by AUTOMATIC1111) formed a complete open-source ecosystem chain. The Reddit r/StableDiffusion community surpassed 250,000 members within a year of SD's release.
- **The first year of AI painting**: In the second half of 2022, AI-generated images transformed from a novelty toy for a handful of researchers into a mass phenomenon—Bilibili tutorials, Xiaohongshu AI painting challenges, cosplay assistance, e-commerce asset generation... "AI painting" became a popular verb. Tencent News, 2023-07-27[^4]

**Divergence from MidJourney**: SD (open-source, controllable, low-cost, requiring technical skill) and MidJourney (closed-source, easy to use, high quality, pay-per-use) formed two poles in the text-to-image domain. SD took the democratization route—anyone could download, modify, fine-tune, and use commercially; MidJourney took the productization route—users paid, the platform delivered aesthetic quality.

### Aftermath

Stability AI subsequently fell into financial and management difficulties. In March 2024, CEO Emad Mostaque resigned. The core R&D team departed to found Black Forest Labs, releasing the FLUX.1 series. By late 2024, Stability AI was acquired. But the open-source ecosystem incubated by SD—LoRA, ControlNet, WebUI, Civitai—has continued to thrive independently of SD itself. Subsequent models like FLUX.1 and SD3.5 have continued the open-source text-to-image lineage.

### Impact

Stable Diffusion's position in the history of AI popularization is analogous to that of SoVITS in the field of speech synthesis:

- **Mass enlightenment about diffusion models**: Hundreds of thousands of users understood for the first time through SD what a diffusion process was—denoising, schedulers, CFG scale, latent space. These concepts seeped from research papers into everyday conversation.
- **The open-source ecosystem paradigm**: SD proved that the combination of an open-source model + plugin ecosystem + community contributions could produce diversity and creativity far exceeding closed-source competitors. The LoRA + ControlNet combination remains a flexibility barrier that closed-source models still struggle to replicate.
- **The birth of "AI-native creators"**: SD gave rise to the first generation of visual creators who used AI tools as their primary creative medium—they were not traditional artists learning AI, but rather people who thought directly in AI, composed with prompts, and controlled precisely with ControlNet. This was a new creative paradigm that did not replace the traditional one but carved out its own territory.
