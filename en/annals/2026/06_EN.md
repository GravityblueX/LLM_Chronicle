# June 2026

## Gemini 3 and Mythos 5: Two Kinds of Dominance, One Kind of Dilemma

> In June 2026, both poles of the large language model industry fired simultaneously: Google's Gemini 3 series continued to lead on multimodal benchmarks, while Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 redefined the meaning of "release" through a tiered safety strategy — only to be halted three days later by a U.S. government export control order.

### Gemini 3 series: Sustained iteration in multimodal dominance

**2026-05-19** — Google released **Gemini 3.5 Flash**, while also updating **Gemini 3.1 Pro**, **3 Deep Think**, and **3.1 Flash-Lite**, forming the most complete product matrix of the Gemini 3 series to date. [^1]

Since its initial release in November 2025, Gemini 3 Pro had maintained its lead across multiple multimodal benchmarks — it was precisely this advantage that triggered OpenAI's "Code Red" emergency response in December 2025 and catalyzed the accelerated release of GPT-5.2 (see *Annals, December 2025*). [^2] Entering 2026, the Gemini 3 series further consolidated its advantages in multimodal understanding and long-context processing through intensive iteration. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis had previously stated that Gemini's design goal was "combining AlphaGo's reasoning capabilities with a large language model's general capabilities" — the Gemini 3 series was the most complete realization of this vision. [^1]

Google's Flash series continued its "high efficiency, low price" strategy: Gemini 3.5 Flash maintained near-Pro-level capabilities while dramatically reducing inference costs, targeting high-throughput enterprise deployment scenarios.

### Claude Mythos 5 and Fable 5: Tiered release and export controls

**2026-06-09** — Anthropic released **Claude Mythos 5** (preview) and **Fable 5** through Project Glasswing. Fable 5 was the "extended safety assurance" version of Mythos, designed for broader use cases. In the release announcement, Anthropic stated that it expected to make "Mythos-class" models available to all customers within weeks, pending additional cybersecurity safeguards. [^3]

This was the first major version update of Mythos since its initial disclosure in April 2026. Project Glasswing's partnership consortium had expanded to **150 organizations across 15 countries** (see *Annals, May 2026*). [^3]

**2026-06-12** — Just three days later, the U.S. Department of Commerce issued an **export control order** to Anthropic, requiring it to prohibit "any foreign national" from accessing Mythos 5 and Fable 5, including "foreign national Anthropic employees." Anthropic determined that the only feasible course of action was to **completely disable** both products — and revoked global access. [^3][^4]

The triggering factor was the government's discovery that Fable 5 might be jailbroken, though Anthropic claimed the vulnerability was "narrow, non-universal" and that other models (including GPT-5.5) were similarly affected. According to *Semafor* and the *WSJ*, **Amazon CEO Andy Jassy's** team conducted red-team testing on Fable 5 and subsequently reported security concerns to the government — Amazon being both Anthropic's largest cloud partner (AWS) and, at this moment, the entity alerting the government. [^4][^5]

This meant: a model designed with "responsible release" as its founding principle was halted by the government on safety grounds just three days after launch. The controlled release framework Anthropic had spent two months building (40 → 150 organizations), Fable 5's extended safety mechanisms — all were rendered null by a government directive. Export controls were applied directly to the global dissemination of an AI model for the first time.

**2026-06-13—15** — The Anthropic team flew to Washington to negotiate with the government, including safety lead **Dave Orr**, frontier red team lead **Logan Graham**, and cybersecurity researcher **Nicholas Carlini**. Concurrently, the public letter website **freefable.org** went online, gathering signatures from technology and cybersecurity executives calling for the revocation of restrictions on Fable 5. [^4]

### The safety paradox further tightened

Mythos 5's export control revocation occurred against the backdrop of Anthropic's ongoing dispute with the U.S. Department of Defense. Since January 2026, the Department of Defense had designated Anthropic as a "supply chain risk" (see *Annals, January 2026*), yet the NSA had continued to use Mythos during this period — the contradiction between the Department of Defense's blacklist and the actual usage by its subordinate agency exposed the policy's disarray. [^3]

In terms of Mythos's security contributions, the limited release since April had already produced significant results: Mozilla used Mythos to discover and patch 271 security vulnerabilities in Firefox; Calif.io employees used Mythos to create a memory corruption exploit affecting Apple's M5 chip. Thomas Fraise of *The Conversation* further warned that Mythos could "destroy nuclear deterrence." [^3]

Meanwhile, China's April 2026 request for Mythos access had been denied. Mistral AI began developing its own safety model because European banks had not received access. [^3]

### Noam Shazeer joins OpenAI

**2026-06-18** — Google Gemini co-lead **Noam Shazeer** left Google to join OpenAI. Shazeer was a co-author of the "Attention Is All You Need" paper and had worked at Google for 20 years; he co-founded Character.AI in 2021, and Google recalled the Character.AI team for approximately $2.7 billion in 2024. Reuters, 2026-06-18[^6]; The Verge[^7]

Shazeer's move was one of the most significant AI talent signals of 2026. He participated in the original design of the Transformer, founded the most closely watched conversational AI startup, and was recalled by Google at a sky-high price — now he chose OpenAI. This transfer carried more symbolic than technical significance: in the same week that Anthropic was confronting the U.S. government, the AI industry's most essential talent flowed to OpenAI.

### Commentary

The large language model industry in June 2026 presented an interesting symmetry: Google maintained commercial dominance through technical iteration, while Anthropic maintained industry attention through safety strategy — but both forms of "dominance" faced their own dilemmas.

Gemini 3 series' problem was not technical — technically, it may have been the strongest multimodal model. The problem was that Google's AI strategy remained deeply embedded in its advertising and search ecosystem. Gemini was not an independent product but a component of the Google empire. Whether its technical lead could be converted into an independent commercial position — after ChatGPT had become synonymous with "AI" and Claude had become synonymous with "safe AI" — remained an open question.

Mythos's dilemma was more acute. Fable 5 was Anthropic's latest answer to the difficult question of "how to responsibly release a dangerous model" — through safety tiering, allowing the high-capability version to operate in controlled environments while the lower-capability version was available to a broader user base. But being halted by export controls within three days demonstrated: **under the current governance framework, AI companies cannot unilaterally define the boundaries of "responsibility."** Governments — and multiple, contradictory government directives at that — hold the final veto.

The irony of this situation was: Anthropic was blacklisted by the Department of Defense for refusing to militarize Claude, while Mythos's export control revocation was because the government feared its exploitation by foreign actors. A single company being simultaneously restricted for "refusing to cooperate" and "being too capable" — this is not regulation, this is chaos. The true state of large language model governance in June 2026 was not "rules too strict" or "rules too lax," but rather **the complete absence of consistent rules**.
