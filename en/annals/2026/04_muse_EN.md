# April 2026

## Muse Spark: The End of Llama and a New Chapter for Meta

> The disappearance of a name sometimes says more than the launch of a new model. In April 2026, Meta announced that it would not release Llama 5 — replaced instead by an entirely new model series: Muse Spark. Llama, the brand that began with a 4chan leak and sustained open-source LLMs for three and a half years, formally reached its end.

### Release

**2026-04** — Meta's **Superintelligence Labs** released **Muse Spark** as the official successor to the Llama series. Meta explicitly stated in its announcement that Muse Spark was an entirely new model series, no longer an "iteration" of Llama. [^1]

This was the first time since Llama 1 in February 2023 that Meta had not named its next flagship model in the "Llama + number" format.

### Why the Llama brand was retired

Llama 5 was never announced. But in the context of Muse Spark's release, the retirement of the Llama brand had been brewing for a long time.

From Llama 1's 4chan leak (February 2023) to Zuckerberg's "open-source manifesto" (July 2024), Llama had been the banner of open-source LLMs. But that banner was severely damaged by the Llama 4 benchmarking controversy (April 2025) — Meta submitted a "specially optimized experimental version" to LMArena for ranking purposes, while the open-source version was noticeably weaker. When DeepSeek-R1 established a new standard of open-source trust through MIT licensing and public chains of thought, Llama's identity as the "open-source flagbearer" had already slipped from Meta's grasp.

This was not a technical problem but a brand problem. The name Llama carried the entire narrative baggage of "open weights with restrictive licensing," "once leaked," "benchmark gaming controversy," and "always a generation behind closed source." If Meta wanted to regain the community's trust, continuing with the Llama 5 brand was unwise — with every release, developers would first ask: "Did they game the system this time?"

The naming of Muse Spark was itself a signal: this is something new. Please look at it with fresh eyes.

### The emergence of Superintelligence Labs

The entity releasing Muse Spark was not "Meta AI" — it was **Meta Superintelligence Labs**. The laboratory's name itself carried a clear message: Meta was redefining its position in the AI race.

Meta's AI narrative had previously been one of "broad availability" — the Llama series' core value proposition was "making large language models accessible to everyone." But the name Superintelligence Labs implied a pivot: Meta was not just building "open-source infrastructure" — it was also vying for the narrative of "cutting-edge capabilities."

This pivot had commercial logic. If Llama was forever "one generation behind GPT," Meta's strategy of open-sourcing Llama — "using open source to erode competitors' moats" (Zuckerberg's own words) — could never be fulfilled. A competitor's moat is not eroded by a model that is perpetually one generation behind. To truly threaten the business models of OpenAI and Anthropic, Meta needed a model that was their equal in frontier capabilities.

Muse Spark was assigned this task. However, Meta had not yet disclosed Muse Spark's detailed parameter scale, architecture information, or licensing terms — these would determine whether Muse Spark was a genuine frontier competitor or simply Llama 5 with a new name.

### Technical approach: Very little is known

As of compilation, Muse Spark's technical details had not been fully disclosed. The following is confirmed information and reasonable inference:

- **Releasing entity**: Meta Superintelligence Labs (not Meta AI).
- **Positioning**: Entirely new model series, not a Llama iteration. This means at least one of the architecture, training strategy, or release strategy has undergone a fundamental change.
- **Relationship with Llama 4**: Llama 4 was the final chapter of the Llama series. There is no lineage relationship between Muse Spark and Llama 4 — at least, Meta left no room for that interpretation in its naming.

As for whether Muse Spark reused Llama 4's MoE architecture, whether it supports ultra-long context, and whether licensing terms were relaxed — these key questions had no answers at the time of compilation.

### Commentary

The end of Llama was not merely an internal product naming decision at Meta. It was a formal period placed on a chapter of large language model history.

Llama's story began with an accident — in February 2023, Meta attempted to control a model's distribution through academic channels, and four days later it was uploaded to 4chan. From that moment on, the history of open-source LLMs was defined by Llama. Llama 2 made "open source" a business strategy rather than an accident. Llama 3.1's 405B parameters and open-source manifesto placed Zuckerberg at the very front of the open-source AI movement. But Llama 4's benchmarking controversy made all of this appear fragile — when the "open-source flagbearer" itself starts gaming rankings, it ceases to be a flagbearer.

The naming of Muse Spark was an honest decision. It acknowledged that the Llama brand had been exhausted and that a new beginning required a new name. But a name is just the beginning. The real test lies in: Can Muse Spark reach the frontier in capability while simultaneously rebuilding the community trust that Llama lost? Or is it merely Llama 5 with a fresh coat of paint, returning to the same cycle of distrust once the community discovers this?

The Llama lineage took three and a half years to complete its full arc from leak to controversy. The era of Muse Spark has just begun — and what it inherits is a battlefield scorched by a trust crisis.
