# May 2026

## Claude Mythos: A Model That Cannot Be Publicly Released, and the Global Panic It Provoked

> Anthropic built a model that can find vulnerabilities in "every major operating system and every major browser." It chose not to release it — opting instead for containment. But containment failed: on the day of the announcement, unauthorized access was obtained through a data leak. A month later, the U.S. government revoked global access on export control grounds.

### Mythos leak and disclosure

**2026-03-26** — Claude Mythos's existence was first made public through a leaked blog draft. Anthropic subsequently confirmed Mythos's development to *Fortune* and stated that the model posed a significant cybersecurity risk. According to *Axios*, Anthropic had warned government officials about Mythos's capabilities earlier that month. Wikipedia, "Claude Mythos"[^1]

**2026-04-07** — Anthropic officially disclosed Mythos. The company stated that it had **no plans to release Mythos to the public** and instead launched **Project Glasswing** — a consortium of enterprises using Mythos to discover and remediate software vulnerabilities. Over 40 companies received access, including Microsoft, Apple, Google, Amazon Web Services, Linux Foundation, Cisco, Nvidia, and Broadcom. [^1]

On the same day, according to *Bloomberg News*, several unauthorized users obtained access to Mythos through a private Discord channel, using information obtained from a recent **Mercor data breach**. [^1]

### Capabilities and impact

Claude Mythos was a large language model specifically designed to discover software vulnerabilities. In the UK AI Security Institute's cyber range testing, Mythos ranked first, Claude Opus 4.6 ranked second, and GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.3 Codex tied for third. [^1]

Anthropic stated that Mythos discovered vulnerabilities in "**every major operating system and every major browser**" during testing. [^1]

Specific achievements:

- **Mozilla** announced two weeks after Mythos's release that it had used the model to discover and patch **271 security vulnerabilities** in Firefox. [^1]
- **2026-05-14**: Calif.io employees announced they had used Mythos to create a memory corruption exploit affecting **Apple's M5 chip**. [^1]

### Global government response

Hours after Mythos's release, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell convened financial executives for a warning briefing. JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, and Morgan Stanley began testing Mythos. The Bank of Canada convened a similar meeting with major lenders the following day. [^1]

Governments responded intensively:

- **2026-04-14**: *Bloomberg* reported that the U.S. Treasury Department was seeking access to Mythos. [^1]
- **2026-04-16**: The White House met with Anthropic regarding Mythos. [^1]
- **2026-04-23**: Indian Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman convened bank and government officials to discuss cybersecurity threats posed by Mythos. [^1]
- **2026-04-24**: Japan's Financial Services Agency held a public-private joint meeting and agreed to establish a working group to address Mythos. [^1]
- **April 2026**: China requested access to Mythos, but was refused by Anthropic. [^1]

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde praised Anthropic's decision to restrict Mythos access. European banks that failed to obtain access prompted **Mistral AI** to begin developing its own safety model. [^1] Thomas Fraise of *The Conversation* warned that Mythos could "destroy nuclear deterrence." [^3]

### GPT-5.5 series expansion

**2026-05-05** — OpenAI made **GPT-5.5 Instant** available as the ChatGPT default model for all **free users**, replacing the previous GPT-5.3 Instant. Wikipedia, "GPT-5.5"[^4]

**2026-05-07** — OpenAI released **GPT-5.5-Cyber**, a restricted preview variant for vetted cybersecurity teams. This was OpenAI's first cybersecurity-specific model — a direct response to Anthropic's Mythos dominance in the domain. [^4]

### Qwen3.7 and Claude Dreaming

**2026-05-18** — Alibaba released the **Qwen3.7** series, comprising Qwen3.7 Max and Qwen3.7 Plus. Wikipedia, "Qwen"[^5]

**Approximately May 2026** — Anthropic introduced a **"Dreaming"** research preview feature for the Managed Agents API, consolidating an agent's persistent memory across sessions by merging duplicates and clearing stale entries. The feature had previously been leaked in March through a Claude Code source code leak under the codename **autoDream**. Wikipedia, "Claude (language model)"[^6]

### Opus 4.8 and the Mythos roadmap

**2026-05-28** — Anthropic released **Claude Opus 4.8** and stated that it expected to make "Mythos-class" models available to all customers within weeks, pending the development of additional cybersecurity safeguards. [^1]

**2026-06-02** — Anthropic expanded Claude Mythos access to **150 organizations across 15 countries**, including multiple institutions in India. [^1]

### Gemini 3 Flash update

**2026-05-19** — Google released **Gemini 3.5 Flash**, the latest update to the Gemini 3 series. By this time, the Gemini 3 series had formed a complete matrix: 3.1 Pro (2026-02-19), 3 Deep Think (2026-02-12), 3.1 Flash-Lite (2026-03-03). Google's Flash series continued its "high efficiency, low price" strategy, dramatically reducing inference costs while maintaining strong multimodal capabilities. [^2]

### Commentary

Claude Mythos was the first time in history that an AI company proactively built a "danger-level" model — and then chose not to release it.

The weight of this decision needs to be understood precisely. Anthropic spent massive compute resources training a model that far surpassed all competitors in the cybersecurity domain. It could have charged exorbitant API fees, locked in the entire security industry, and gained a decisive competitive advantage over OpenAI and Google. Instead, it chose containment — making the model available only through Project Glasswing to a controlled enterprise consortium.

This was not a technical decision but a **governance decision**. It set a precedent: some models are dangerous enough that they should not be publicly released. The question is — who defines "dangerous enough"? When China's request for access was denied, when the U.S. government revoked global access through export controls, when Mistral AI began building its own model because European banks were excluded — every participant was answering this question through action, but the answers diverged.

The paradox of Mythos lies in this: its value derives precisely from its danger. The ability to find vulnerabilities in "every major operating system and every major browser" means it can also be used to create attacks. Mozilla used it to patch 271 vulnerabilities; but the same capability in the hands of an attacker would mean those 271 vulnerabilities were not "patched" but "weaponized." The two-week window — from discovery to patch — might be the distance between security and catastrophe.

The June 12 export control revocation pushed the question into even sharper relief: when the U.S. government simultaneously blacklists Anthropic at the Department of Defense level while restricting Mythos's global dissemination at the export control level, which government directive should Anthropic obey? On the governance question of AI safety, we are still in a lawless frontier.
