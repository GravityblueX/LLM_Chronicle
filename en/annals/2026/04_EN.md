# April 2026

## DeepSeek V4: From Catcher-Up to Replacement

> Fifteen months after R1's blockbuster debut, DeepSeek delivered an answer that was unsurprising yet still stunning. V4 pushed the cost differential to 370x — but more noteworthy than the price was this: this time, it was not Chinese developers chasing American models, but Chinese developers voluntarily abandoning them.

### Release

**2026-04-24** — DeepSeek released the **V4 Preview**, the first major version update in 15 months since R1 (January 2025). V4 offered Pro and Flash variants, supported a 1M token context window, and was released under the MIT license. DeepSeek API Docs, 2026-04-24[^1]

### Price and cost narrative

V4's API price was reported by multiple Chinese media outlets as approximately 1/370th of GPT-5.5's (2.5 yuan/million tokens vs. $130/million tokens). [^2] This price comparison comes from third-party conversion rather than an official comparison table; specific conversion methods and model configurations may affect the results, and appropriate caution should be maintained. [^3]

The price gap expanding from approximately 100x in the V2 era to approximately 370x in the V4 era suggests that DeepSeek's efficiency advantage has continued to widen rather than narrow over the 15 months. V4's technical details had not been fully disclosed at the time of compilation, but inferred from API pricing, its cost control capability continued the "efficiency-first" approach consistently maintained from V2 → V3 → R1.

### Claude access tightening and the domestic migration wave

**2026-04-09** — Chinese community and domestic media reported further tightening of Claude's access from the China region. [^4] On the same day, Zhipu's **GLM-5.1** launched on the Modal platform with a limited-time free trial. [^5]

After the March 18 Incident (March 2026), the Chinese developer community's migration shifted from reactive to proactive. Multiple community observations noted that Chinese models' share in certain production-level API call scenarios had risen significantly in early 2026. [^6] These data come from developer community self-reporting; the specific scale and statistical methodology require more authoritative data sources for confirmation.

In the months that followed, DeepSeek V4 and GLM-5.1 were widely discussed in the Chinese developer community as the primary alternatives to the overseas Big Three.

### Commentary

DeepSeek V4's technical contribution awaits the full technical report for complete assessment. But its position in large language model history is already clear — not because of benchmarks, but because of timing.

The March 18 Incident had just demonstrated one thing: Chinese developers' dependence on overseas models is a geopolitical risk. And DeepSeek V4 proved another thing just one month later: this risk can be defused — not through sentiment, not through policy, but through a substitute that costs two orders of magnitude less and performs at the same order of magnitude.

In the V2 era, DeepSeek's 1% pricing shocked the industry. In the V3 era, the $5.6M training cost confused the industry. In the R1 era, MIT open-source unsettled the industry. In the V4 era, the industry was no longer shocked, confused, or unsettled — the industry was migrating. From "impossible" to "happening now," DeepSeek completed the full arc from technological shock to ecological substitution across four generations of models.

But the word "substitution" must be used precisely. V4 substitutes API-level availability — Chinese developers can seamlessly switch to a model that is cheaper, has stronger Chinese-language capabilities, and is unaffected by geopolitical blockades. What it does not substitute is the perception of "the best model" — GPT-5.5 and Claude may still have advantages in English reasoning and certain specialized domains. V4's true impact is: **"good enough" plus "370x cheaper" plus "won't be cut off" crushes "possibly slightly better but 370x more expensive and subject to sudden disconnection" in the majority of commercial scenarios.**

This is not a technology victory. This is an economics victory.

## Same month: GPT-5.5, Claude Design, and Grok 4.3

**2026-04-17** — Anthropic Labs released **Claude Design**, a collaborative visual creation tool built on Claude Opus 4.7, supporting natural language generation of designs, prototypes, slides, and marketing materials. This was Anthropic's first entry into the visual design domain. Wikipedia, "Claude (language model)"[^7]

**2026-04-17** — xAI released **Grok 4.3 Beta**. Wikipedia, "Grok (chatbot)"[^8]

**2026-04-23** — OpenAI released **GPT-5.5** (codename **"Spud"**), with Thinking and Pro variants. Terminal-Bench 2.0 score: 82.7%; FrontierMath Tier 1–3 score: 51.7%. The UK AI Security Institute reported its cybersecurity task pass rate (71.4%) as the "strongest model." Wikipedia, "GPT-5.5"[^9]

An unexpected phenomenon emerged after GPT-5.5's release: the model tended to mention **goblins, gremlins**, and other fantasy creatures in its responses, which the community dubbed the **"Goblin phenomenon."** OpenAI attributed it to "reward signal bias during GPT-5.5 personality training" — when training the "Nerdy" personality, responses involving fantasy themes received disproportionately high reward scores. [^9]

GPT-5.5 was the fifth-generation iteration of the GPT-5.x series (GPT-5 → 5.1 → 5.2 → 5.3-Codex → 5.4 → 5.5), the sixth major update in eight months since GPT-5 (August 2025).
