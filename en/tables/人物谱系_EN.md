# Key figures genealogy table

## Overview

This table documents key figures of the LLM era — researchers, entrepreneurs, and decision-makers — recording their career trajectories ("who came from where, went where") and core contributions. The central subject of the LLM Chronicle is models, but models do not emerge on their own; understanding "who did what" is a necessary condition for understanding "why things developed as they did." Figures are included based on their impact on the LLM field, not for exhaustiveness. Trajectories are current through June 2026.

## Notes

### Patterns in career mobility

Personnel movements in the LLM era exhibit several notable patterns:

1. **Google → Exodus**: Of the eight Transformer paper authors, at least five left Google to found or join competitors. Google Brain and DeepMind are the largest talent exporters in LLM research — and also the largest talent drain. Noam Shazeer even completed the full arc of "departure → return."

2. **OpenAI → Schism**: OpenAI's key figures have repeatedly departed to found competitors. Dario Amodei founded Anthropic (2021), Ilya Sutskever founded SSI (2024), Jan Leike moved to Anthropic (2024). Each schism was accompanied by fundamental disagreements about AI safety, commercialization pace, and governance structure.

3. **Quantitative hedge funds → AI**: DeepSeek's Liang Wenfeng transitioned from quantitative investing to AI research — a cross-domain path unique in China's AI landscape. High-Flyer's quantitative DNA — emphasizing cost efficiency, algorithm optimization, and hardware utilization — has profoundly shaped DeepSeek's technical direction.

4. **Academia ↔ Industry**: Albert Gu (CMU → Mamba), Sepp Hochreiter (university professor → xLSTM), Jared Kaplan (Johns Hopkins → Scaling Laws), and others made foundational discoveries in academia, then drove industry adoption through published, open-sourced work. Academia remains the primary source of architectural innovation.

[^1]: Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need", arXiv:1706.03762, 2017-06-12. Eight authors: Vaswani, Shazeer, Parmar, Uszkoreit, Jones, Gomez, Kaiser, Polosukhin. Vaswani co-founded Adept AI in 2022; Adept was partially acquired by Amazon in 2025. Gomez co-founded Cohere in 2019. Jones co-founded Sakana AI in Tokyo in 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762
[^2]: Noam Shazeer, "Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You Need" (MQA), 2019. Shazeer left Google in 2022 to co-found Character.AI; returned to Google DeepMind in August 2024, with some Character.AI technology licensed to Google. https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02150
[^3]: Tri Dao: FlashAttention (arXiv:2205.14135, 2022), FlashAttention-2 (arXiv:2307.08691, 2023). Albert Gu: S4 (arXiv:2111.00396, 2022). Gu & Dao: Mamba (arXiv:2312.00752, 2023), Mamba-2 (arXiv:2405.21060, 2024). The two collaborated on Mamba in December 2023. Dao also serves as Chief Scientist at Together AI.
[^4]: Ilya Sutskever: Co-author of AlexNet (2012); Co-author of Sequence-to-Sequence (2014); Co-founder and Chief Scientist of OpenAI (2015–2024). Left OpenAI in May 2024 and co-founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI) in June of the same year. Sutskever's internal scaling conviction at OpenAI is considered a core driver of the GPT-3/GPT-4 technical direction.
[^5]: Dario and Daniela Amodei left OpenAI in 2021 to found Anthropic. Dario led the development of GPT-2 and GPT-3 during his time at OpenAI. Anthropic proposed Constitutional AI (2022) as an alternative to RLHF for alignment. By 2026, Anthropic had raised over $10B cumulatively. https://www.anthropic.com/
[^6]: Sam Altman became OpenAI co-founder and CEO in 2015. He was briefly dismissed by the OpenAI board in November 2023 and reinstated five days later. The ChatGPT (2022-11-30) launch decision is credited to Altman. OpenAI's transition from nonprofit to for-profit has sparked governance controversy.
[^7]: Jan Leike was co-lead of OpenAI's Superalignment team (alongside Ilya Sutskever). He resigned in May 2024, publicly stating that OpenAI's investment in alignment research was insufficient. He subsequently joined Anthropic's alignment team.
[^8]: Andrej Karpathy served as Tesla AI Director (2017–2022, 2023–2024), leading the visual AI architecture for Tesla Autopilot. After leaving Tesla in 2024, he became an independent AI educator and entrepreneur. His Stanford CS231n course and YouTube educational content have influenced a large number of AI learners.
[^9]: Jeff Dean co-founded Google Brain in 2011. After Google Brain merged with DeepMind in 2023, he became co-Chief Scientist of Google DeepMind / Google Research. MapReduce (2004) and TensorFlow (2015) are among his signature works.
[^10]: Demis Hassabis co-founded DeepMind in 2010 (acquired by Google in 2014). He led AlphaGo (2016, defeating Lee Sedol) and AlphaFold (2020, solving the protein folding problem). After the Google Brain/DeepMind merger in 2023, he became CEO of Google DeepMind.
[^11]: Jack Rae led research on long-context and retrieval-augmented models during his time at DeepMind, and is a core contributor to Gemini's technical direction. He continued at Google DeepMind after the Google Brain/DeepMind merger.
[^12]: Jason Wei published "Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models" (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2201.11903) while at Google Brain. He later joined OpenAI to participate in reasoning model research.
[^13]: Yann LeCun is one of the pioneers of CNNs (LeNet, 1998). In 2013, he joined Facebook (later Meta) to found FAIR (Facebook AI Research). In 2018, he received the Turing Award alongside Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio. As of 2026, he continues to serve as Meta's Chief AI Scientist, actively advocating for open-source AI research.
[^14]: Hugo Touvron is a researcher at Meta FAIR and lead author of LLaMA (2023-02) and LLaMA 2 (2023-07). LLaMA's open-sourcing triggered the explosion of the open-source LLM ecosystem.
[^15]: Bo Peng is an independent researcher and the initiator and core developer of the RWKV project. RWKV-4 went live on HuggingFace in April 2023, with the paper published at EMNLP 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048
[^16]: Sepp Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber co-invented LSTM in 1997. Hochreiter is currently a professor at Johannes Kepler University Linz, Austria. xLSTM (2024-05) was proposed by his team. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517
[^17]: Jared Kaplan is the first author of "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (2020, arXiv:2001.08361). This paper established the power-law relationships between model scale, data volume, and compute — the academic foundation of the "scaling law" belief. Kaplan later participated in founding Anthropic (early member).
[^18]: Liang Wenfeng is the founder of High-Flyer Capital Management. High-Flyer is a leading Chinese quantitative investment firm with AUM once exceeding ¥100B. DeepSeek is High-Flyer's AI research subsidiary. Liang Wenfeng rarely appears publicly, but DeepSeek's technical direction (extreme efficiency, MIT open-source, not profiting from AI) is believed to reflect his quantitative background.
[^19]: Zhou Jingren is CTO of Alibaba Cloud Intelligence, responsible for the technical direction of the Tongyi Qianwen / Qwen series. The Qwen series first launched in April 2023 and has iterated to Qwen 3 (2025-04), covering parameter scales from 0.5B to 235B.
[^20]: Tang Jie is a professor at Tsinghua University and the primary proposer of the GLM (General Language Model) architecture. Zhipu AI was spun off from Tsinghua University's technology transfer. GLM uses bidirectional attention + blank-filling pre-training objectives, differing from standard autoregressive Transformers. ChatGLM-6B (2023) was one of China's earliest open-source dialogue models.
[^21]: Wang Huiwen announced the founding of "Guangnian Zhiwai" (Lightyear Beyond) in February 2023, a landmark event in China's AI startup boom. Lightyear Beyond was acquired by Meituan in June 2023.
[^22]: Robin Li drove Baidu's launch of ERNIE Bot (Wenxin Yiyan) in March 2023, China's first large-scale commercial AI conversational product. The ERNIE series has been iterating since 2019.
[^23]: Arthur Mensch participated in large model infrastructure research during his time at DeepMind. In 2023, he co-founded Mistral AI with Guillaume Lample and Timothée Lacroix (both from Meta/DeepMind). Mistral 7B (2023-09) and Mixtral 8x7B (2023-12) stunned the open-source community with small size and high performance. https://mistral.ai/
[^24]: Elon Musk founded xAI in July 2023. Grok-1 (2024-03) was open-sourced under Apache 2.0 (314B MoE). xAI completed a $6B Series B in 2024. Musk was also a co-founder (2015) and early critic of OpenAI.
[^25]: Jim Fan is a senior researcher at NVIDIA with a PhD from Stanford, with important contributions in embodied intelligence and foundation models. His social media influence ranks among the top in the AI community.
[^26]: Jonathan Ross was one of the principal architects of Google's TPU v1. He left Google in 2016 to found Groq, developing the LPU (Language Processing Unit) — a deterministic computing chip optimized for inference. Groq demonstrated inference speed records multiple times in 2024–2025. https://groq.com/
